## Silent Cards
Silent cards are gained from Card Reward screens during games played with the Silent. They can also be gained from merchant shops and from the effects of events, relics, and other Silent cards. Silent cards come in three rarities: common, uncommon, and rare.

Information for upgraded cards is shown in parentheses. Ex: A Thousand Cuts deals 1 damage, A Thousand Cuts+ deals (2) damage.

## Acrobatics
1.  Tips

-  Acrobatics costs 1 energy to draw 3 cards and discard 1 card, thus yielding a net gain of 2 cards (not considering the cost of drawing acrobatics in the first place). Although on the surface it may seem to be no better than a card like Backflip, it synergizes very well with cards whose effects trigger upon being discarded (e.g. Tactician, Reflex), or who have additional effects if other cards have been discarded (e.g. Sneaky Strike, Eviscerate). It also allows you discard Curses, Statuses, or cards that aren't useful on a given turn.
  - The upgrade for Acrobatics is relatively powerful, as it provides a net gain of 3 cards instead of 2. In addition to the obvious benefits of additional card draw, this also means that you have more options for cards to discard.
-  In general, drawing 3 cards sounds very meritable, but this is usually only true in the later part of the game. Early on, when you only have 3 Energy, it is rarely reasonable to spend 1 energy purely to draw cards, since you still need energy to play the cards that you draw.
  - Reusable cards that provide energy can help improve the effectiveness of Acrobatics early on. For instance, Concentrate (especially when both it and Acrobatics are upgraded) becomes more viable when you have the additional cards drawn by Acrobatics.

## After Image
stacking_behavior = Intensity
After Image is a Rare Power for the Silent.  Whenever the player plays another card, they gain 1 Block immediately afterwards.  When Upgraded, After Image becomes Innate.

    1.  Mechanics

-  Dexterity does not affect the Block gained from After Image, as the Block is generated by a buff and not the card itself.
- The After Image buff is stackable, so playing multiple copies of After Image will provide even more Block per card. Since After Image+ only costs 1 Energy and is Innate, playing more than 1 copy on the first turn is easily done.

    1.  Tips

-  After Image is helpful for countering the Corrupt Heart's Beat of Death status, which inflicts 1 damage immediately after the player plays a card.
-  This card is an excellent addition to any Shiv-based deck, as every individual Shiv grants 1 Block.
-  Playing many cards in a turn (e.g. with Shivs) is liable to generate excess Block, so Blur can be used to carry it over to the next turn.

## Alchemize
Alchemize is a Rare Silent card that generates a random potion upon use. It costs 1 Energy, and when Upgraded costs no energy. As a card that can potentially heal the player, it cannot be generated by random card generation such as Distraction and Dead Branch.

It can't generate Fruit Juice (due to a design rule against generating healing), but it can generate Regen Potion and Fairy in a Bottle.

    1.  Interactions
- Positive
  - Potion Belt allows you to save more potions between battles.
  - Toy Ornithopter makes each potion heal 5 HP.
  - Sacred Bark doubles the effectiveness of potions.
- Negative
  - Sozu makes Alchemize useless.
  - Ascension 11+ gives 1 fewer potion slots, making it harder to stockpile potions.

- Weekly Patch 6: We're Back!

    1.  Trivia

## All-Out Attack
All-Out Attack is an Uncommon Card for the Silent which deals damage to all enemies once, then discards a random card in your hand. If after playing All-Out Attack your hand is empty, no cards will be discarded.

    1.  Strategy
All-Out Attack is a strong source of upfront AoE (Area-of-Effect) damage. It has higher burst of damage than Dagger Spray, upgraded or otherwise. It scales with Strength worse than the latter, given that All-Out Attack hits only once. However, due to the same reason, it is less punishing to use against enemies with Thorns.

With its discard being random in nature, it is highly preferable to use all the other high-impact cards before using this card as a sort of 'closer' card to make it less likely that it will discard a card that you actually wanted to play.

    1.  Synergies

-  Tactician and Reflex grant benefits when discarded, although you need to plan your use of card carefully to have All-Out Attack more likely to discard said cards.
-  Tough Bandages and Tingsha have a synergy with discarding, granting free Block and damage respectively.
-  This card is stronger in Act 1, where enemies have low health.
  -  It can still perform moderately well in Act 2, being a cheap burst of AoE damage.


-  Less risky to use against Spiker than Dagger Spray, especially if there are more than one. However, the Silent has an access to much better indirect damage in a form of Poison.



    1.  Trivia
-  The card's artwork, a cloud with fists popping out suggesting rapid beatings, is a reference to the move of the same name from the Persona series.

## Backflip
Backflip is a common Skill for The Silent. It costs 1 Energy, generates 5 Block, and draws 2 cards when played.  Upon upgrade, it generates 8 Block instead.

The amount of Block this card generates is the same as a Defend, but the card draw keeps this card relevant in the later stages of a run.

## Backstab
Backstab is an Uncommon Card for the Silent. It deals damage and exhaust. As an Innate card, it is always drawn on the first turn, taking one spot on your first card drawn.

    1.  Strategy
Backstab deals a single burst of damage to a single enemy. It does not cost Energy, essentially giving you a single-use free attack.

With it being Innate, it is guaranteed that you will draw on the first turn, and is included on your draw count. This could be useful on lower floors, as Backstab has a respectable damage against enemy with lower health, but it could cripple you on higher floor since it could take a spot for a potentially more powerful card. Unless you have a appropriate set of Relics, it is not advised to take more than one copy of this card.

    1.  Synergies

- Bag of Marbles: Amplifies Backstab moderate damage up to a more respectable amount, allowing you to one-shot a low-health enemy.
- Akabeko: Similar to Bag of Marbles, Akabeko increases Backstab damage to a respectable amount. While Backstab hits only once, it is a guaranteed first turn draw due to its Innate nature, giving you a guaranteed burst of damage on the first turn.
- Bag of Preparation, Ring of the Snake, and Ring of the Serpent: The additional card draw lessens the drawback from Innate cards taking a space on your first draw.
- Gambling Chip and Gambler's Brew: Allows the player to discard and draw a new card in place of Backstab in case that it is not beneficial.
- Dead Branch: With Backstab having 0 cost, Dead Branch turns it into free damage and a card generation, lessens the drawback from its Innate nature by giving you a chance to get a more beneficial card for free.
- Shuriken and Kunai: Backstab gives you a reliable counter on these relic for free on the first turn. Along with some Attack cards you could potentially drawn on the first turn, they could gives you a momentum you need to scale the fight.
-  Can help you take down minions of Gremlin Leader and Reptomancer in order to steer them to waste a turn summoning more minions.


-  A strange strategy with Shuriken and Kunai comes from the fact that Backstab is free attack guaranteed to be drawn in the first turn, and taking 3 copies of them while having either of those relics guarantees that you could trigger Strength and/or Dexterity on the first turn for free while also dealing a burst of 33-45 damage. While those bonus can help you scale the fight, having a psuedo-Ninja Scroll that takes up 3 cards of your initial draw makes it more risky on a higher floor with more aggressive and punishing enemies.

    1.  Trivia
-  The Beta Art for this card depicts a Caesar salad, because Julius Caesar is a historical figure who was famously betrayed and stabbed to death by his senators on the Ides of March.

    1.  Update History
- Weekly Update 10: Quality of Life
  - Backstab buffed: Cost lowered to 0. Damage decreased to 11.

## Blade Dance
Blade Dance is a Common Skill for the Silent. It creates 3(4) Shivs and places them in your current hand.

    1.  Strategy
Blade Dance is one of the most common ways to obtain Shivs for the Silent. Since multiple Shivs are created, cards boosting Shivs or multiple attacks are very effective with Blade Dance. Creating 3 or 4 Shivs is extremely powerful when combined with boosting effects such as Accuracy, Envenom, Strength, etc. As a Common card, Blade Dance is easy to find, allowing for the deck to get a few copies of it in order to boost Shiv bonuses even more. Even without boosting Shivs, Blade Dance alone is good enough damage in Act 1.

## Bouncing Flask
1.  Strategy
Bouncing Flask is a good source of single-target poison damage, as it adds 9 (12) Poison total if there is one enemy.

Bouncing Flask is less effective when there are multiple enemies, both because each enemy gets less Poison and because the Poison is applied randomly.

    1.  Interactions

-  Each bounce counts as a separate application of Poison. This means each bounce can remove 1 Artifact, apply additional damage from Sadistic Nature (up to 28 damage if both are upgraded), or add the bonus +1 poison from Snecko Skull (+3/+4 additional poison).

## Bullet Time
Bullet Time is a Rare Skill for the Silent. It inflicts No Draw status on the player, preventing them from drawing any cards for the rest of the turn, and all cards in hand have their costs reduced to 0 Energy.

When Upgraded, Bullet Time costs 2 Energy instead of 3.

    1.  Mechanics

- The No Draw status can be negated by Artifact, or removed with Orange Pellets.
-  No Draw only prevents you from drawing cards from your deck; it will not prevent you from generating cards via e.g. the Dead Branch or Shiv-creating cards.

    1.  Strategy
-  Taking full advantage of Bullet Time requires more thought and effort than it might seem at first. The key thing to remember is that Bullet Time itself takes up a slot in any hand you draw it into, and does nothing but let you play the remaining cards. Furthermore, unlike with generic energy-gain cards, cards that draw other cards must be played before Bullet Time itself.
-  If you're just drawing five cards a turn, Bullet Time will let you play four of them; if you already have an Energy relic, and most of your cards cost 1 or 0, Bullet Time will be completely useless (no better than a Curse) every time it's drawn. For it to be useful, the total cost of the other cards worth playing in your hand has to be higher than your Energy limit. In a normal deck that hasn't been intentionally built around it, this happens less frequently than you would expect.
  - Alternatively, instead of running a high-cost deck, you must at least ensure you have enough card-draw effects that you can use before Bullet Time consistently, specifically Acrobatics or Backflip. This requires 4 Energy or Bullet Time+. Other draws such as Prepared or Tools of the Trade aren't necessarily sufficient, since they don't give a net positive in the amount of cards you get. Escape Plan only replaces itself and may grant some Block, but does not increase the number of cards in your hand.
-  As a result of this, the decks that want Bullet Time are ones with lots of high-cost cards and few cards that cost 0 or even 1. It's also more valuable if you've made it to the late game and still haven't found any suitable Energy relics, forcing you to accomplish as much as you can with your starting 3.
-  Curses and Statuses can weaken Bullet Time further; any deck that uses it is going to be vulnerable to them.

## Burst
stacking_behavior = Counter

Burst is a Skill card for The Silent. It plays the next Skill card this turn a second time, for no Energy. As a Rare card, it can be obtained after defeating a Boss. It is The Silent's version of Double Tap and Amplify.

Upon upgrade, it will trigger again on the 2nd Skill card played in the same turn.

The second play is NOT added to your draw or discard pile.

The scaling buff from Burst (and also from Double Tap and Amplify) is the number of cards that will be played twice, not the number of times the card will be played. This means playing multiple Bursts will add to the number of skills that will be played twice that turn, not make the next skill play 3 or more times.

    1.  Interactions

-  The second play will not cost any Energy, but it will trigger any other consequences of playing the card. For example, a Bursted J.A.X. will trigger the self-damage twice.
-  The second play of a card that costs X energy will use the same value of X as the first.
-  The second play acts as a separate card. This has interactions including, but not limited to:
  -  Ink Bottle, Letter Opener: Both plays count towards playing skills for their benefits.
  -  Velvet Choker: Both plays count towards the amount of cards played for the turn.
  -  Time Eater's Time Warp power: Both plays increase the counter as usual. If the second skill is the 13th action on his counter, it will NOT occur, unlike Velvet Choker.

-  The second play happens after the Skill is sent to the draw, discard or Exhaust piles.
-  The second play can trigger any Exhaust synergies, but only if the Skill Exhausts when played.
  -  In other words, the copied Skill "not going to your discard pile" does NOT count as an Exhaust by itself.

- Weekly Patch 42: Reboot
  - Updating icon for Burst power.
- Weekly Patch 1: The First of Many!

## Calculated Gamble
Calculated Gamble is a Uncommon Skill card for the Silent. When played, the player's current hand is discarded and that many cards are drawn, effectively shuffling the player's hand.

When Upgraded, Calculated Gamble no longer Exhausts.

    1.  Interactions
-  Calculated Gamble works well with cards that have effects upon being discarded, such as Reflex and Tactician. It also plays well with cards that have their energy cost decreased upon the discarding of other cards, such as Sneaky Strike and Eviscerate.
- Calculated Gamble synergizes well with Tingsha and Tough Bandages, dealing three damage to a random enemy and adding three Block to the player respectively for every discard.
- Calculated Gamble does not synergize well with cards that enable Retain, such as Well-Laid Plans, as Calculated Gamble can discard a valuable card.
- Calculated Gamble's upgrade can be a large priority, since it's effect can be the core of specific decks.

    1.  Update History

  - Calculated Gamble card nerfed.

    1.  Trivia

  -  This could be an oversight, or dice in the Spire universe simply work differently.

## Catalyst
1.  Mechanics

Technically, Catalyst add 1x or 2x the enemy's current Poison value. This can cause unexpected interactions.

-  Artifact blocks Catalyst's Poison.
-  Snecko Skull adds 1 after Catalyst's poison.

    1.  Strategy
Catalyst allows poison decks to output much needed burst damage, at the cost of being slightly unwieldy. Practically a necessity for pure poison decks to beat the Act 3 boss.
-  Catalyst synergizes with Nightmare, allowing much greater scaling of poison damage.
-  Catalyst synergizes with Well-Laid Plans, Runic Pyramid, since they reserve Catalyst in hand to be used at the optimal turn.

Act 3 bosses have counter-mechanisms for poison damage. The Awakened One and Time Eater dispel every debuff (including poison) at phase 2. Donu and Deca are not a single entity and have Artifact.

So tactical usage of Catalyst is needed. Especially when it's Time Eater, Recommendation is using Catalyst at a turn which is phase 1 and you can kill it in 2 turns.

## Corpse Explosion
stacking_behavior = Intensity

Corpse Explosion is a Rare Skill for The Silent that applies Poison to one enemy and causes it to inflict a huge amount of damage to its companions upon death.

Upgrading Corpse Explosion increases the Poison from 6 (21 total damage) to 9 (45 total damage).

    1.  Mechanics
-  Applies 2 distinct debuffs in that order: poison and "Corpse Explosion".  So, an enemy with one artifact will only block poison.
-  The "Corpse Explosion" effect lasts indefinitely (unless the enemy clears its debuffs).
-  The fatal damage can come from any source--it does not have to come from Poison.
-  It can stack, allowing an enemy to deal two or more times its max HP to all foes.
-  Because the poison and explosion damage is dealt after Block is removed, very few enemies can block it.
-  Corpse Explosion does trigger when Reptomancer's Daggers automatically die on their second turn.

    1.  Strategy
Corpse Explosion is an exceptionally strong card when fighting multiple enemies. Even without the above-average amount of Poison, it can inflict massive amounts of area-of-effect damage and easily clear boards.

As a Poison enabler, it's strong but clunky. 9 Poison is the single largest printed amount of Poison, but two Energy is a big investment. Consider it a step up from Bouncing Flask in most situations.

A key decision when casting Corpse Explosion is whether to target an enemy with low HP to wipe it off the board earlier, or to target the enemy with the highest max HP to wipe the entire board at once. Generally, targeting the beefiest enemy and then focusing all of your attacks on it is the optimal play, though if a shuffle is imminent, it may be smarter to pick a weaker enemy and try to stack the effect again.

Corpse Explosion is an excellent workhorse for defensive decks. The Poison cuts through Block while the explosion scales the damage up, allowing you to fully focus on defense. It also negates up to two artifacts because of its two effects (poison and apply poison upon death).

Upgrading Corpse Explosion is recommended to increase its potency against single enemies.

    1.  Synergies
-  Burst allows Corpse Explosion to apply a huge amount of Poison and area-of-effect damage.
-  Corpse Explosion triggers Sadistic Nature twice.
-  The card synergizes with all Poison-matters cards like Bane and Catalyst. The large amount of Poison makes Corpse Explosion a particularly strong setup for the latter.

    1.  Trivia
This card is most likely a reference to the Diablo II Necromancer Corpse Explosion ability; in addition to have the exact same name, the mechanics of the card are also the same (deal damage to other enemies based on the health of the target)

## Malaise
Malaise is a Rare card for The Silent which permanently reduces one enemy's Strength and applies many turns of Weak. As an X-cost card, it consumes all remaining Energy when played.

Strength Reduction is applied before Weak, and will be negated by Artifact in that sequence. Enemies with a single Artifact charge will only have Weak applied on them, as Strength Reduction gets negated by the Artifact. 2 or more Artifact charges will completely nullify this card's effect, removing 2 Artifact charges in the process.

Upgrading Malaise increases the Strength loss and Weak count by 1. This will trigger even if you play Malaise with 0 Energy remaining, effectively applying 1 Strength Loss and 1 Weak for no cost.

    1.  Strategy
Malaise is a deceptively strong card. It has a potential to be the strongest source of permanent Strength reduction. Its Strength reduction severely punishes the Snake Plant, Hexaghost, and other enemies that rely on multi-strike, low damage attacks. Its Weak applied can also cripple a hard-hitting enemy for many turns.

The Silent has access to a number of Energy-increasing cards, which can power Malaise up further.

    1.  Synergies

-  Well-Laid Plans can Retain Malaise until a turn with respectably generated Energy.
-  Burst interacts with Malaise the same way as any other X-cost card: Malaise will be played twice at X Strength, where X is the amount of Energy remaining.
  -  For example, playing Burst with 3 energy, then playing Malaise (with 2 energy), will reduce Strength by 4 and apply 4 Weak (barring Artifact). This is particularly powerful with Chemical X, as the bonus application is applied twice.
-  Malaise applies two debuffs for the purpose of Sadistic Nature.
-  Ice Cream helps the Silent conserve energy between turns, further increasing Malaise's strength.
-  Chemical X overperforms with this card, potentially reducing an enemy's Strength by two or three points for free.
-  Paper Krane cripples the enemy even further with empowered Weak, softening even a single, hard-hitting attack to be more manageable.
  -  Malaise can also applies a longer-lasting Weak with a single use, making Paper Krane even more effective in a longer fight.
-  Bag of Marbles, Red Mask, and Twisted Funnel help clear innate Artifact charges to allow Malaise to be used with its full potential.
-  Hexaghost, Snake Plant, and Byrd are severely crippled by this card, as they mostly rely on multi-hit attacks.

## Prepared
Prepared is a Skill for the Silent. When played, you draw 1 card, then discard 1 card of your choice.

When Upgraded, you draw 2 cards and discard 2 cards instead of 1.

    1.  Strategy

-  Prepared is a low-risk pick. Even though it is a net disadvantage in terms of card quantity, this is only a problem if you are sure you can play every card in your hand most of the time. However, be wary of taking too many copies of Prepared, as multiple uses in a single turn will unnecessarily discard more than is likely necessary.

-  Upgrade to Prepared+ as soon as possible. The additional draw lets you see more cards and assemble stronger plays, and the additional discard enables cards like Eviscerate and Tactician more effectively.

    1.  Synergies
Prepared is a key component of any discard deck - it draws you closer to the relevant cards, enables them, and lets you discard cards at will, all while leaving your entire Energy reserve available.

Prepared is especially strong with Tactician, Reflex, Sneaky Strike, and Eviscerate, efficiently drawing and enabling them in one move.

## Sneaky Strike
Sneaky Strike is an Attack card for The Silent that can deal damage at effectively zero Energy. Upgrading Sneaky Strike increases the damage dealt from 12 to 16.

    1.  Strategy
Sneaky Strike is a standard act 1 damage card. It is usually worth it with one or two discard effects in addition to the base deck.

Most Silent deck will benefit from the addition of draw manipulation like Acrobatics or Prepared, and Sneaky Strike can be part of the damage solution. At uncommon Eviscerate will usually be preferred as it fills the same role more efficiently.

When playing with Sneaky Strike, play slowly and sequence your moves carefully. Accidentally playing Sneaky Strike before your discard enabler can be a fatal mistake.

-Remember that Sneaky Strike alone is basically a 2*Strike. If the deck is built around draw&discard, Sneaky Strike can be a decent 12/16 damage with 0 energy. But it WILL take up your draw slot and is very weak if no discard card is drawn. If cards that discard other cards such as Survivor is played, but no draw cards are played, even if you play Sneaky Strike, you may find yourself holding only 2 remaining cards, and the energy gain wasted.

    1.  Synergies
Sneaky Strike requires a huge number of discard effects to function, as well as card draw to find it.

Tools of the Trade makes this much easier to play, because it guarantees a discard every turn for the remainder of combat. However, it won't help you if you draw Sneaky Strike before (or on the same turn as) the power.

Reducing the card's cost with Enlightenment, Madness, or Snecko Eye makes it generate Energy, enabling very high-tempo plays.

Like 0-cost cards, Sneaky Strike should be accompanied by card draw or expensive cards to make the most of the 'refunded' Energy.

Decks with Calculated Gamble to accelerate draw can sprinkle in Sneaky Strike for a bit of extra damage.

    1.  Trivia
Early in development, Sneaky Strike was named Underhanded Strike, an apt name given the tender spot being pummeled in the card's art.
