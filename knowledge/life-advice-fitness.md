## Cooking for Gamers

### 10 Quick One-Pot Meals (Under 30 Minutes)

**1. Chicken Stir-Fry Rice Bowl** — Cook rice, dice chicken thighs (thighs > breast for flavor), toss in a hot pan with soy sauce, sesame oil, garlic, frozen veggies. One pan, 20 minutes. The trick is getting the pan screaming hot before the chicken goes in so you get actual sear instead of sad steaming.

**2. Pasta Aglio e Olio** — Boil spaghetti. While it cooks, slowly toast sliced garlic in olive oil (low heat, do NOT burn it). Toss pasta in with a ladle of pasta water, red pepper flakes, parsley. Literally 4 ingredients. This is the "it's 1 AM and I need real food" meal.

**3. Chili** — Brown ground beef or turkey, dump in canned tomatoes, beans, chili powder, cumin, garlic, onion. Simmer 20 min. Makes like 6 servings so you're set for days. Top with cheese and sour cream.

**4. Chicken Curry (Lazy Version)** — Dice chicken, cook in oil with onion. Add a jar of curry simmer sauce (Patak's or store brand tikka masala). Add a can of coconut milk if you want it creamier. Serve over rice. Tastes like you spent an hour, took 20 minutes.

**5. Quesadilla Stack** — Tortilla in a pan, cheese, leftover whatever (chicken, beans, peppers), more cheese, another tortilla. Flip when golden. Cut into wedges. This is meal prep leftovers reborn.

**6. Egg Fried Rice** — Day-old rice (crucial — fresh rice gets mushy), scramble eggs in a hot pan, push to the side, add rice, soy sauce, sesame oil, frozen peas and carrots. The key is using cold leftover rice and a very hot pan.

**7. Sausage and Peppers Skillet** — Slice Italian sausage, cook with sliced peppers and onions. Add canned tomatoes. Eat with bread or over pasta. Dead simple.

**8. Black Bean Tacos** — Canned black beans heated with cumin, chili powder, garlic. Tortillas, cheese, salsa, sour cream. Done in 10 minutes. Cheap and actually filling.

**9. Ramen Upgrade** — Instant ramen but make it real food: soft boil an egg (6.5 minutes), add frozen veggies, a splash of soy sauce and sesame oil, sliced green onion. Goes from sad to legitimate in 2 minutes of extra effort.

**10. Sheet Pan Sausage and Veggies** — Cut sausage and whatever vegetables you have (potatoes, broccoli, peppers, onion) onto a sheet pan, toss with olive oil and seasoning, bake at 400F for 25 minutes. Zero active cooking time.

### Meal Prep Basics

The Sunday Prep system: pick 2 proteins, 2 carbs, 2 vegetables. Cook them all in bulk. Mix and match through the week. Rice cooker is the single best investment — set it and forget it. Buy a pack of meal prep containers (glass > plastic, doesn't stain or warp).

Good prep proteins: chicken thighs (oven at 400F, 25 min), ground turkey/beef (brown a whole pound), hard boiled eggs (12 min boil, ice bath). Good prep carbs: rice (rice cooker), roasted potatoes, pasta. Good prep veggies: roasted broccoli, roasted sweet potatoes, raw bell peppers and cucumbers for snacking.

Everything gets better with sauce. Keep soy sauce, hot sauce, ranch, salsa, and some kind of vinaigrette around. Same chicken and rice tastes like 5 different meals with different sauces.

### Healthy Gaming Snacks

The problem with chips and candy is the crash — blood sugar spikes then tanks and your reaction time goes with it. Better options:

- **Trail mix** — nuts, dried fruit, maybe some dark chocolate chips. Sustained energy, no crash.
- **Greek yogurt with granola** — protein and carbs, keeps you full.
- **Hummus and veggies/pita** — actually tastes good and doesn't leave grease on your peripherals.
- **Beef jerky** — high protein, shelf stable, no mess.
- **Fruit** — apples, bananas, grapes. Simple. Effective. Bananas are basically nature's energy bar.
- **Popcorn (air-popped or light butter)** — way less calorie-dense than chips, still satisfies the crunch craving.
- **Rice cakes with peanut butter** — sounds boring, actually solid.
- **Cheese and crackers** — protein + carbs, no cooking.

Hydration matters more than snacks. Keep a big water bottle at your desk. Dehydration tanks cognitive performance before you even feel thirsty. If water is boring, throw in some Mio or Crystal Light.

### Basic Cooking Skills

**Rice** — Rinse until water runs mostly clear (removes excess starch). 1:1.5 ratio rice to water for white rice, 1:2 for brown. Bring to boil, reduce to low, cover, 18 min for white / 45 min for brown. Don't lift the lid. Or just get a rice cooker for $20 and never think about it again.

**Pasta** — Big pot of water, generous salt (it should taste like mild ocean water), rolling boil before pasta goes in. Stir within the first minute so it doesn't stick. Cook 1-2 minutes less than the box says for al dente. SAVE A CUP OF PASTA WATER before draining — it's liquid gold for making sauces stick.

**Eggs** — Scrambled: low and slow with butter, constantly stirring, pull off heat when they're slightly underdone (carryover heat finishes them). Fried: medium heat, butter or oil, crack in gently, cook until whites set but yolk is still runny. Hard boiled: cold water, bring to boil, cover and remove from heat, 12 minutes, ice bath immediately.

**Stir-Fry Fundamentals** — Hot pan, small batches (overcrowding = steaming), cut everything the same size so it cooks evenly. Aromatics (garlic, ginger) go in last 30 seconds or they burn. Basic sauce: soy sauce + sesame oil + rice vinegar + cornstarch slurry to thicken.

**Knife Skills** — Curl your fingers on the guide hand (the "claw grip") so you're pushing with knuckles, not exposing fingertips. A sharp knife is safer than a dull one because it goes where you point it instead of slipping. Learn to dice an onion properly and you're ahead of 90% of home cooks.

### Budget Cooking Tips

- Buy in bulk: rice, beans, oats, pasta, frozen vegetables. These are pennies per serving.
- Frozen vegetables are nutritionally identical to fresh and don't go bad. No guilt.
- Chicken thighs are half the price of breasts and taste better. Bone-in skin-on is cheapest.
- Canned beans and tomatoes are staples. Stock up when they're on sale.
- Store brand everything. The generic canned tomatoes are the same tomatoes.
- An instant pot or slow cooker turns cheap tough cuts of meat into amazing food. Chuck roast for $5/lb becomes pot roast that falls apart with a fork.
- Plan meals around what's on sale, not the other way around.
- Eggs are one of the cheapest complete proteins that exist. Learn to cook them multiple ways.

## Fitness for Desk Workers

### The Big Compound Lifts

These are the movements that give you the most return on time invested. If you only have 3 hours a week to train, compound lifts are what you do.

**Squat** — Bar on your upper back (not your neck), feet shoulder width, toes slightly out. Brace your core like someone's about to punch your stomach. Push your knees out over your toes as you descend. Hit at least parallel (hip crease below knee). Drive through midfoot to stand up. Common mistakes: knees caving in (push them out), leaning too far forward (keep chest up), not hitting depth (check your ankle mobility).

**Deadlift (Conventional)** — Bar over midfoot, hands just outside knees, shins touch the bar. Flatten your back (neutral spine), chest up, take the slack out of the bar before you pull. Think about pushing the floor away, not yanking the bar up. Lock out by squeezing your glutes, NOT by hyperextending your lower back. Common mistakes: rounding the lower back (this is how you get hurt — deload and fix it), jerking the bar off the floor (smooth pull), bar drifting away from your body (drag it up your shins).

**Bench Press** — Retract your shoulder blades (squeeze them together and down, like you're putting them in your back pockets). Arch your upper back slightly. Feet flat on the floor. Grip width so your forearms are vertical at the bottom. Bar touches mid-chest, not your neck. Drive through your feet as you press. Common mistakes: flaring elbows to 90 degrees (tuck them to about 45), bouncing the bar off your chest, losing the shoulder blade retraction.

**Overhead Press (OHP)** — Bar on front delts at collarbone height, grip just outside shoulders. Brace your core and glutes. Press straight up, move your head back slightly so the bar travels in a straight line, then push your head through once the bar passes your forehead. Common mistakes: leaning back excessively (turns it into an incline press and loads your spine), not locking out fully overhead, flaring the rib cage (squeeze your abs).

**Barbell Row (Bent Over)** — Hinge at the hips so your torso is roughly 45 degrees to the floor. Grip just outside knees. Pull the bar to your lower chest/upper belly. Squeeze your shoulder blades at the top. Lower with control. Common mistakes: using momentum/body English to swing the weight up (ego lifting), not pulling high enough (rowing to your belly button means you're mostly using biceps), standing too upright.

### Beginner Workout Splits

**Starting Strength / StrongLifts style (3 days/week)** — Best for true beginners. Two workouts alternating:
- Workout A: Squat 3x5, Bench 3x5, Row 3x5
- Workout B: Squat 3x5, OHP 3x5, Deadlift 1x5
- Monday A, Wednesday B, Friday A, next week flip. Add 5 lbs every session. This works for 3-6 months before you need something more complex.

**Upper/Lower Split (4 days/week)** — Good intermediate progression:
- Upper: Bench, Row, OHP, Curls, Tricep pushdowns
- Lower: Squat, Deadlift or Romanian Deadlift, Leg press, Leg curls
- Mon Upper, Tue Lower, Thu Upper, Fri Lower.

**Push/Pull/Legs (6 days/week)** — For when you're hooked and want to live in the gym:
- Push: Bench, OHP, dips, lateral raises, triceps
- Pull: Rows, pullups, face pulls, curls
- Legs: Squats, deadlifts, lunges, leg curls, calves
- Run it twice per week.

The best split is the one you'll actually show up for consistently. 3 days of a beginner program done consistently beats a 6-day PPL you bail on after 2 weeks.

### Stretching Routine for Gamers (Do This Daily, 10 Minutes)

**Wrists (critical for keyboard/mouse/controller):**
- Prayer stretch: palms together in front of chest, push down until you feel the stretch in your forearms, hold 30 seconds.
- Reverse prayer: backs of hands together, push up. Hold 30 seconds.
- Wrist circles: 10 each direction.
- Finger extensions: spread all fingers wide, hold 5 seconds, repeat 10 times.

**Neck:**
- Ear to shoulder stretch: tilt head to one side, gentle pressure with same-side hand. 30 seconds each side.
- Chin tucks: pull your chin straight back (making a double chin). Hold 5 seconds, 10 reps. This counteracts forward head posture from staring at screens.

**Back and Shoulders:**
- Doorway chest stretch: forearm on door frame at 90 degrees, step through. 30 seconds each side. Opens up the chest that gets tight from hunching.
- Cat-cow: on hands and knees, alternate between arching and rounding your back. 10 reps. Good for general spinal mobility.
- Thread the needle: on hands and knees, reach one arm under your body and rotate. 30 seconds each side.

**Hips (sitting destroys hip mobility):**
- 90/90 stretch: sit on floor with both legs at 90 degrees, front leg and back leg. Lean forward over the front leg. 30 seconds each side.
- Couch stretch: back foot on a couch or chair behind you, lunge forward. Destroys hip flexor tightness. 30 seconds each side.
- Deep squat hold: just sit in a deep squat with feet flat for 30-60 seconds. If you can't do this, that's a sign you really need to.

### Ergonomics

**Monitor:** Top of the screen should be at eye level. If you're looking down, your neck is taking the hit for 8+ hours. Use a monitor arm or stack textbooks under it. Distance should be roughly arm's length.

**Chair:** Feet flat on the floor (or a footrest). Knees at roughly 90 degrees. Lumbar support — if your chair doesn't have it, a rolled-up towel works. Don't sit on the edge of your chair; use the back support, that's what it's there for.

**Keyboard and Mouse:** Elbows at roughly 90 degrees. Wrists neutral (not bent up or down). If you're reaching for your mouse, it's too far away. Consider a wrist rest for the keyboard but don't rest your wrists while actively typing — only during pauses. Keyboard tilt should be flat or negative (feet retracted), not tilted up toward you.

**The 20-20-20 Rule:** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Your eyes focus differently at distance and this prevents eye strain. Set a timer if you need to.

**Get Up and Move:** Every hour, stand up and walk around for 2-5 minutes. Your body is not designed to be static for 8 hours. Even just standing and doing 10 bodyweight squats is better than nothing.

### Cardio Basics

You don't need to run marathons. The minimum effective dose for cardiovascular health is about 150 minutes per week of moderate activity (brisk walking counts) or 75 minutes of vigorous activity (running, cycling, rowing).

**Walking** is criminally underrated. 30 minutes of walking daily does more for your overall health than most people realize. It's low impact, doesn't require recovery, improves mood, and you can do it while listening to podcasts or music. 8,000-10,000 steps per day is a solid target.

**Zone 2 cardio** (you can hold a conversation but it's not easy) is the most efficient for building cardiovascular base. 30-45 minutes, 2-3 times per week. Stationary bike, incline treadmill walking, or swimming.

**HIIT** is time-efficient but taxing on recovery. 1-2 sessions per week max. 20-30 seconds all-out effort, 60-90 seconds rest, repeat 8-10 times. Total workout time: 15-20 minutes.

Don't skip cardio because you're afraid of "losing gains." That's a myth perpetuated by people who don't want to do cardio. It improves recovery between sets, improves sleep, and keeps your heart from quitting on you at 50.

### Common Form Mistakes and Fixes

- **Ego lifting:** The number on the bar doesn't matter if your form is garbage. Nobody in the gym is watching you or judging the weight. Drop the weight 20%, do it right, and you'll actually progress faster because you're training the muscle instead of momentum.
- **Half-rep squats:** If you're not hitting parallel, it doesn't count. Drop weight, work on ankle and hip mobility, use a box to gauge depth.
- **Rounded back deadlift:** This is how herniated discs happen. Film yourself from the side. If your lower back rounds, deload immediately.
- **Bench press with no arch or retraction:** Flat back bench puts your shoulders in a compromised position. Retract the scapulae.
- **Holding your breath the wrong way:** You should be bracing (Valsalva maneuver — big breath into your belly, brace like you're about to get punched) NOT just holding air in your chest. This stabilizes your spine under load.
- **Ignoring warm-up sets:** Don't walk in and load your working weight. Do the empty bar, then 50%, then 70%, then working sets. Your joints and nervous system need the ramp-up.

## Life Skills

### Sleep Hygiene

Sleep is the single most impactful thing you can optimize for cognitive performance, mood, physical recovery, and reaction time. None of the gaming skill practice matters if your brain is running on fumes.

**Blue light:** Screens suppress melatonin production. Ideally, no screens 1 hour before bed (realistically, use Night Shift / f.lux / blue light glasses). The phone doomscrolling in bed is the biggest offender. Put the phone across the room so you have to physically get up to grab it.

**Consistent schedule:** Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, including weekends. Your circadian rhythm doesn't know it's Saturday. Sleeping in on weekends creates "social jet lag" that makes Monday feel worse. If you need to catch up on sleep, go to bed earlier rather than sleeping later.

**Room environment:** Cool (65-68F / 18-20C is optimal), dark (blackout curtains or sleep mask), and quiet (earplugs or white noise machine). Your body temperature needs to drop to initiate sleep — a hot room fights this.

**Caffeine cutoff:** Caffeine has a half-life of 5-6 hours. If you have coffee at 4 PM, half that caffeine is still in your system at 10 PM. Set a hard cutoff at noon or 2 PM at the latest.

**The 90-minute rule:** Sleep cycles are roughly 90 minutes. Waking up mid-cycle feels awful (groggy, disoriented). Time your sleep in 90-minute blocks: 6 hours, 7.5 hours, or 9 hours. This is why 6 hours sometimes feels better than 7 — 7 hours interrupts the cycle.

### Time Management / Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro method: 25 minutes of focused work, 5 minute break. After 4 cycles, take a 15-30 minute break. The key insight is that knowing a break is coming makes it easier to resist distractions during the work interval. It's not about forcing focus — it's about making focus finite and therefore manageable.

During the 25 minutes: phone on Do Not Disturb or in another room. Close Discord, close Reddit, close YouTube. One task, full attention. During the 5 minutes: actually step away from the screen. Stand up, stretch, refill water.

If you can't do 25 minutes, start with 15. The point is building the habit of deliberate focus, not hitting a specific number.

**Task prioritization (Eisenhower Matrix):**
- Urgent + Important: Do it now.
- Important + Not Urgent: Schedule it. This is where most valuable long-term work lives and gets ignored.
- Urgent + Not Important: Delegate it or batch it.
- Neither: Delete it.

Most things that feel urgent aren't important. Most things that are important don't feel urgent. Recognizing this distinction is half the battle.

### Basic Personal Finance

**The 50/30/20 Rule:** 50% of after-tax income on needs (rent, food, utilities, minimum debt payments), 30% on wants (gaming, eating out, subscriptions, hobbies), 20% on savings and extra debt payments. If you can't hit these numbers exactly, just knowing the framework helps you identify where your money is going.

**Compound Interest:** This is the most powerful force in personal finance. $100/month invested at 8% average annual return (roughly the stock market's historical average) becomes:
- After 10 years: ~$18,400
- After 20 years: ~$58,900
- After 30 years: ~$149,000
- After 40 years: ~$349,000

Starting at 22 instead of 32 is the difference between retiring comfortably and scrambling. The best time to start was yesterday. The second best time is now.

**Emergency fund:** 3-6 months of expenses in a high-yield savings account (not under your mattress, not in crypto). This prevents one bad month from becoming a financial spiral. Build this before investing.

**Debt priority:** Pay minimums on everything, then throw extra money at the highest interest rate debt first (avalanche method). Credit card debt at 20%+ APR is an emergency — no investment consistently beats that rate.

**Investing basics:** For most people, a target-date index fund or a simple three-fund portfolio (US stocks, international stocks, bonds) through a 401k or IRA is all you need. Don't try to pick individual stocks until your retirement savings are on autopilot.

### Dealing with Tilt / Mental Health Basics

**Tilt recognition:** The moment you feel anger, frustration, or the "I need to queue again immediately to prove it was just bad luck" impulse — that's the signal to stop. You're not going to play better while tilted. You're going to play worse and compound the frustration. Step away for 15-30 minutes minimum. Walk. Hydrate. Reset.

**The 10-minute rule:** When you feel the tilt coming, commit to 10 minutes away from the game. Not alt-tabbed to Reddit — physically away from the screen. After 10 minutes, if you genuinely feel reset, play. If you're still irritated, extend.

**Cognitive reframes for competitive gaming:**
- A loss is data, not an identity statement. "I lost" is not the same as "I'm bad."
- Rank is a lagging indicator. If you're improving fundamentals, the rank will follow.
- Bad teammates and bad luck average out over hundreds of games. The only constant is you.
- Focus on process goals (I will check the minimap every 5 seconds) not outcome goals (I will hit Diamond).

**When it's more than tilt:** Persistent low mood, loss of interest in things you usually enjoy, difficulty sleeping or sleeping too much, feeling hopeless — these aren't just "tilt." This is worth talking to someone about. Therapy isn't just for crisis situations — it's maintenance for your brain, like going to the gym for your body. Most universities offer free counseling. BetterHelp and similar platforms exist for post-school. There's no weakness in getting professional help; the weakness is refusing to.

**Basic stress management:** Exercise helps. Sleep helps. Social connection helps. Sunlight helps. Reducing caffeine and alcohol helps. These aren't platitudes — they're literally the first-line interventions in clinical psychology because they work. If your lifestyle has none of these, fixing that comes before any productivity hack or supplement stack.

### Social Skills for Introverts

**Introversion is not social anxiety.** Introversion means social interaction drains your energy and solitude recharges it. That's a preference, not a disorder. Social anxiety is fear and avoidance of social situations. If it's the latter, see the mental health section above.

**Small talk is a skill, not a talent.** It can be practiced and improved like anything else. The key: ask questions and listen. People love talking about themselves. "What have you been playing lately?" is an infinite conversation starter in gaming circles.

**The 3-second rule:** When you think of something to say in a conversation, say it within 3 seconds. The longer you deliberate, the more you talk yourself out of it, and the more awkward the silence becomes.

**Online friendships are real friendships.** Don't let anyone tell you otherwise. But also make sure you have at least some face-to-face social interaction regularly. Voice chat in Discord is a middle ground that counts.

**Energy management:** If you know a social event is coming, schedule alone time before and after. Don't stack social obligations back to back. It's okay to leave early. "I had a great time but I'm going to head out" is a complete sentence that doesn't require justification.

**Being interesting:** Read things, try things, have opinions. The easiest way to be interesting in conversation is to be interested in a wide variety of things. Even passively consuming different content (documentaries, podcasts, books) gives you more to contribute to conversations.
