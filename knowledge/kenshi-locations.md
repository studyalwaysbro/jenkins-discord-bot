## Major Towns
Major Towns are locations where many different types of Vendors can be found. Often, Major Towns will contain more than one Bar. Major Towns tend to be large settlements with many buildings and residents, although not all of them provide the same services, and their size and population can vary wildly.

They often have a large selection of stores and multiple buildings for sale. They are also the only items on the map that can be seen at any zoom level, besides your own settlements.

    1.  The Holy Nation
The zealous fanatics of the Holy Nation inhabit these towns. They are not safe for non-human Races without Human escort. Even then you may encounter trouble with the residents and guards. Skeletons should stay away at all times, as they are killed on sight regardless of any escort.

    1.  Bad Teeth
-  A fortress city guarding a mountain pass between the fertile valley of Okran's Pride and hostile wilderness of the Skinner's Roam. Dust storms and hungry bandits laid waste to the land east of Bad Teeth, but the city remains a place for respite and safety.

    1.  Blister Hill
-  The glorious capital of the Holy Nation and the seat of Holy Lord Phoenix. The areas around here are patrolled heavily by The Holy Nation, and as such are relatively free of any bandit presence. If you are a full Human squad, the very fertile areas around here are great for setting up a base.

    1.  Stack
-  A large town just southeast of the center of the map, on the edge of The Holy Nation's territory. It has a decent variety of shops, and its surrounding areas often have Starving Bandits, Wild Dogs, and Dust Bandits. With all of the relatively weak enemies it makes for a decent training area for newer characters. Its proximity to the Western Hive and The Hub makes it a good location for aspiring traders and thieves alike.

    1.  Shek Kingdom

    1.  Admag
-  The Shek capital and home of the Stone Golem. It is located on top a hill in the northern part of the Stenn Desert. It features almost all types of shops and bars. There also is a Shinobi Thieves tower. Many 10 percent quality Copper mines dotted outside town.

    1.  Squin
-  A town nestled in a narrow canyon. It is located in the western part of the Border Zone. There are two  bars alongside most basic shop types. The town has relatively safe access to high quality Iron and Copper resource nodes.

    1.  United Cities

    1.  Drifter's Last
-  A large town controlled by the United Cities faction, located in the region of the map known as The Hook. The town has a number of farms in the surrounding area, though the area is frequently attacked by Outlaw Farmers. The town is frequently visited by traveling merchants, as well as Slave Hunters and Manhunters.

    1.  Catun
-  City in Bonefields. Contains a weapons shop controlled by the Catun Scrapmasters.

    1.  Clownsteady
-  A compact town controlled by the United Cities faction, located in the Southwest region of the map known as The Hook.

Often times they will not have the amenities usually associated with Major Towns, such as beds for rent and stores to buy and sell items at. Although some do.

## Nest
Nests are locations that can spawn in the wilds of Kenshi. They are home to a variety of the world's Fauna (with the exception of Fogmen and Fishmen), such as Bonedogs, River Raptors & Beak Things among others. Nests are recognizable on the map by a paw print mark. Nests have many bones and clutter. They can occasionally have loot such as Animal Skins, Weapons, or Beak Thing Eggs (found in Beak Thing nests). It is possible to wipe out the population of a nest. When this happens, a notification will appear and the nest will de-spawn after a certain time, before spawning new nests in replacement.

## Town Overrides
Town Overrides are new locations which spawn to replace other locations. Town Overrides are a result of a World State change.

Many places are able to become DEAD when all or enough inhabitants have been killed. This is different than when a location falls into ruins due to a Town Override.

If more than one override becomes available at the same time, the one with the most world states on it will take priority. And if there is a tie, the highest one in the override list will be selected.

Any buildings purchased by the player will not be destroyed/lost due to Town Overrides.

## World of Kenshi
Zones  Locations  Nests and Camps  Town Residents

The Kenshi world map is vast and multicolored. Kenshi has many zones with natural, earthy designs which are at odds with more outlandish areas like Leviathan Coast, Gut, and Venge.

Kenshi has many non-player locations, of sizes ranging from a single building to large cities. The game includes a world states system which creates reactions to deaths of notable people. These reactions to power vacuums can result in new locations spawning in or towns being taken over by new factions.

Zones in Kenshi are not just aesthetic, as they have specific soil types, resource availability, and seasons. These factors come into play when players are constructing outposts. Even without building anything, weather can give players pause, as harmful weather effects can occur all across the map.

Throughout development, the world of Kenshi went through many changes. For information on Old World locations prior to the map overhaul, see Old World.

    1.  Trivia
- Kenshi takes place on a tidally locked moon, similar to Earth's moon, meaning the same side of the moon is always the visible side.

Treasure seekers should be wary, as the ruins have not gone altogether silent. The mechanical sentries of the past still roam the rubble, and those who wish to salvage the lost tech from these labs must face down many Security Spiders. Tech Hunters have scouted these locations and mapped them for brave treasure seekers.

The town was likely destroyed during The Holy Nation's initial invasion into Bast or the ensuing stalemate, however it since been partially recovered by the United Cities, now acting as the largest military outpost in Bast.

It may have once been one of Bast's trade towns, however it has since been repurposed into a military outpost by United Cities Samurai, though it still keeps its old bar.

This appears to be a place where the United Cities station and organize much of their patrols throughout Bast, as the status of Drin is tied with Lord Inaba and Emperor Tengu, directly correlating with homeless patrol spawns throughout the region.

## Drowned Ruins
The Drowned Ruins are housed in the crumbling remains of a long-forgotten settlement, the lost Second Empire town of Catch, located midway up the river that separates Rebirth from the Hidden Forest.

Though half sunken into the water, the ruins of Catch are not altogether abandoned. A sole skeleton merchant who refers to himself as the Armor King makes his home in the Drowned Ruins along with his royal guard and three of his own personal Thralls.

Details on the Armour King's merchandise can be found on his shop page. Unlike all other Armour Shops, beds can be rented upstairs for free. The existence of regular beds instead of Skeleton Repair Beds only creates questions.
    1.  Tips

-  It's not recommended to steal from this shop because all of the NPCs have combat stats past 90 (and you should respect the Armour King).

    1.  Tips
-  The best way to unlock the front gate is from the side of the staircase, rather than going directly in front of the gate itself. From this position, you're less likely to be seen (you're behind the main wall, not the transparent gate), and are less open to crossbow fire from the turrets. You can even open the gate from here without drawing any alarm, allowing your entire group to enter the base without resistance.
-  Upon entering the gate, you will most likely be swarmed. With low level characters, you will no doubt be wiped, and once downed the Dust Bandits will pick you up and imprison you inside of cages on the third floor. The Dust King will either be outside, and can easily be lured to a safer place away from the tower, or he will be inside and will be one of the last bandits to come after you. In the case of the former, you can get him to chase you far away from the aggro range of the others. Otherwise, you will have to fight through the rest of the bandits, which can easily result in a wipe and party deaths.
-  It is possible to claim the Dust King's bounty very early in the game if one hires a team or two of Mercenaries to storm the tower for them. Simply unlock and open the gate and enter with your hired blades, and wait for him to go down to collect the bounty. The disadvantages of this are that a single character is unlikely to be able to collect all of the loot from the tower, and missing out on the experience from both Dust Bandit raids, and the fight itself.

## Empty Lab
Empty Labs are Ancient Locations which can be found in The Crater. These Labs are non-interactive and do not contain lootable items.

## Empty Ruins
Empty Ruins are locations which contain no loot. These damaged structures serve as cosmetic world building tool to create a greater sense that Kenshi was once a thriving place but time has changed.

Examples of these can be found in Bonefields, Floodlands, South Wetlands, Stenn Desert, and The Swamp.

## Endin
Endin is a ruined city found in Bast under control of the United Cities. Along with cities of Bast and Drin, Endin was largely destroyed by the Holy Army. Similarly to Bast and unlike Drin, it has no Samurai guards and is solely inhabited by peasants, exiles and outlaws. It can be found by exploration or by acquiring and reading the Map of the United Cities (Central Territories).

The only intact building is a storm house often occupied by a few vagrants or exiles from the United Cities. They are fairly harmless and unlikely to be hostile unless you are hostile to the United Cities yourself. The camp beds inside are free for your squad to use. There are few junk items located inside the building.

## Exile Camp
Exile Camp is a small encampment located Southwest of Squin and directly south of the Stenn Desert Waystation. It's the only location controlled by the Band of Bones. Notable residents include Tora the Fearless.

If the camp is approached, some exiles will run out to chase the player (or any other faction's) party away.

The camp features Camp Beds, Tents and MKIII Training Dummies.

## Expedition 4
Expedition 4 is a ruined town built and abandoned by the Tech Hunters. There are lots of items on ground in and around the ruined buildings. Unlike most ruins, this location actually has a working wind generator.

This location is referenced in Tech Hunter's Notes: The Ashlands. In many ways, it is similar to the Expedition 5 location. Although the number implicates that there were 3 expeditions before it, it is however, not (at least explicitly) stated in the game what the results of those expeditions were.

## Expedition 5
Expedition 5 is a location similar to Expedition 4. It is a partially ruined town with lots of components on ground around buildings. It can be found east of Spring and south of Reaver Camp.

This location was created by the Tech Hunters during their fifth expedition south. This location is referenced in Tech Hunter's Notes: The Ashlands. Although the number implicates that there were 4 expeditions before it, only the 4th one has an observable abandoned settlement, it's not explicitly stated in the game what the results of the other expeditions were.

## First Village
First Village is a secluded Cannibal Village found in the far north of Darkfinger. It is the home of Meat Lord, his lieutenants and many other Cannibals.

It is comprised of various metal shacks and tents surrounding a central collection of cages. One shack is home to Meat Lord and several Cannibal Chiefs. They will stand in a static position, while the rest of the village's population will continuously roam around the center.

It is the largest of the Darkfinger villages, with it being a relatively dangerous location due to how many Cannibals swarm the village center and the abundance of roaming squads that use it as a base.

However, unless you wish to claim Meat Lord's bounty, there is not too much reason to visit the village due it having relatively poor loot for the danger that it poses.

    1.  Trivia

-  Its name could imply that it was the original village of the Cannibals, although this is contradicted by some dialogue.

## Flotsam Safehouse
Flotsam Safehouse is a single building located to the north of Rebirth. It was established as a way point for escaped Rebirth Slaves to rest and recover their strength. ## Heng
Heng (Zone)

Heng is a United Cities settlement built on top of a rocky plateau, located southwest of Heft and east of Tengu's Vault. Heng is connected to its satellite-city, the Trader's Edge, by a metal bridge, giving access to the Traders Guild HQ. This makes it the main trading town for the Empire and therefore well defended. You can find Heng simply through exploration or by acquiring and reading the Map of the United Cities (Central Territories).

It relies upon shipments from Stone Camp, South Stone Camp and Eyesocket. Therefore, it is supported by more slave camps than any other Major UC town, making it the town with the most stable local economy in the UC.

Lord Yoshinaga can be found in the Noble House, being in it is considered trespassing. He is protected by many Hooded Empire Noble Guards.

This town is noteworthy for having a Thieves Guild where the player could buy equipment for sneaking, and a Robotics Workshop where characters in need of robotic repairs and replacements can go to.

The player can often time encounter Harmothoe's trade caravan in Heng or its neighboring city, Trader's Edge.

There is a true wonder of the Desert region found in the mountainous area just East of Heng - a small and beautiful lake.

## Hive Village
A Hive Village is a minor location controlled by the Western Hive. Currently there are five villages located in Vain as well as three in Dreg.

Hive Villages in Vain are often attacked by Beak Things and Gorillos. Players who are being pursued by these animals can go into Villages for protection. However, since the Hive is not an ally of the player, hivers will only enter the fight if the animal attacks a drone as well.

Hive villagers don't trade with player's hive characters because they are considered 'hiveless'. So, to trade with the hive, players must have a character of another race (human, skeleton, or shek). Hive Traders will not sell goods to any member of the player faction if they find out that there is a hiveless character in the player's squad.

    1.  Buildings

-  Hive Robotics Shop

    1.  Village Inhabitants
Worker drones act as labourers: cooking, mining, sleeping etc. They don't have any weapons. Therefore, they try to flee if they face an enemy. However, if there are no more soldier drones around, workers can steal weapons from player's unconscious characters and try to fight the enemy.

Hive traders are princes and act as usual traders. They don't sleep and can fight.

Soldier drones act in two ways: as town guards and as shop guards. Town guards fight with enemies to defend the village while shop guards will fight only to protect their shop and hive trader. In their free time, town guards patrol town and sleep. Shop guards do not leave their shops. All soldier drones are armed with an ancient quality Polearm, Rag Loincloth and a Basic First Aid Kit.

## Mongrel
Shinobi GuardMongrel, the sole settlement of eponymous faction, is an outlaw town located in the middle of the Fog Islands. It is an isolated town located in the middle of nowhere made up of various shady individuals lucky enough to safely reach there.

Due to its location, it can be extremely dangerous to attempt to reach it. Players may lose characters to the Fogmen on the journey if not careful. Many Holy Nation Outlaws try to make their way to Mongrel, although they are often killed and devoured by the surrounding horde of Fogmen.

In the current time, the town itself is an outlaw haven composed of various criminals such as Shinobi Thieves, and a few Tech Hunters, the vast majority of which are stranded on the isolated island. The town has been hinted to run low on food supplies.

## Mourn
Mourn Barman
Mourn is a Major Town located in the Bonefields between Catun and Flats Lagoon. Mourn is built inside of an ancient metal structure, looming over the north and south sides of the town. A slave rebellion left this once thriving mining town in shambles. There are no guards at the gates, and most buildings are ruined.  There are several Harpoon Turrets on the walls but due to the fact that Mourn has no energy-producing structures, they are unable to be used unless the player faction builds their own power. Mourn is probably the most vulnerable non-hostile town in all of Kenshi. If measures to help the town are not taken, the place will be overrun and slaughtered by the sheer savagery of the wildlife that lurks outside its walls, including the dreaded Beak Things. Even then, it's likely Mourn's walls will be filled with piles of dead bodies on a constant basis.

Mourn's lack of gate guards makes it susceptible to all the potential spawns in the Bonefields. These dangers can be Beak Things, Bonedogs, Boneyard Wolves, and Skin Spiders. Patrols of Band of Bones and Gorillo Bandits are also possible. It is unlikely that a Leviathan will attempt to enter the town, but one could potentially be lured there.

There are many camp beds scattered inside the walls of this town. Players can use them at no cost. Players should remain focused on resting characters, however, because characters might not wake up to protect themselves from being eaten alive by Beak Things.

## Rebirth
Paladin, introducing the player to Rebirth

Rebirth is a labor camp in the pit of a large mountain where The Holy Nation sends criminals for punishment and repentance. All races can be present here, except for Skeletons, as The Holy Nation does not consider them to have a soul. It is located in an eponymous region north of main Holy Nation territory.

This place functions as a concentration camp for religious re-education and penance, where the imprisoned are to perform various labour tasks and to build and maintain a statue resembling either Okran or Phoenix to redeem themsleves until their passing, by which point their soul would then be believed to be reborn as a child of Okran, although it's suggested that the sentencing for some is temporary and could be let out once they are deemed to be cleansed sufficiently.

Much like other prison camps in the HN, this camp is established for religious reasons and a place to organize prisoners. The camp houses various undesirables such as hardened criminals and border trespassers, the prevalence of Sheks in this area suggests that many were raiders  of various Shek factions such as Shek Kingdom, Kral's Chosen and Berserkers.

Players can sell or donate characters to Rebirth Shopkeep they consider in need of punishment. Non-player characters, such as Fishmen, Cannibals or Bandits, will be accepted for a price of c.500 if the player specifically asks for compensation, otherwise it will yield +1 reputation with The Holy Nation. Unlike the similar practice of Slave Traders (and their close associates), Rebirth isn't meant for personal gain or exploitation but rather for re-education and discipline, as a result the player wouldn't be able to buy any inmates.

Players can potentially gain recruits by unlocking cages and shackles, depending on the personality of the inmate in question.

You can start your game in Rebirth with two non-Skeleton characters if you choose "The Slaves" game start.

## Sniper Tower
Sniper Tower has many Upgraded Watchbot Skeletons manning MkII Harpoons at the top of the tower.

Interestingly, they are all facing northwards - preventing players from using the pits as an easy access point to the Ashlands.

Be aware that like all turrets, these can fire in nearly 360 degrees. The first character or two reaching the roof will likely be hit with multiple harpoons. Heavy armor with 100% coverage is recommended. Oftentimes they will be able to hit you through the floors with their ranged weapons.

If you are able to clear the tower (or are particularly brave), the 3rd floor contains Beds with the usual 8x healing multiplier for organic characters. Unfortunately, only one bed can be used due to junk placement, but it's possible to clip characters in by picking them up.

Beware! The liquid surrounding this area is acidic.

The Hub is in the Border Zone, a generally uninhabited region between the Shek Kingdom and the Holy Nation. The history of the Hub is written in a series of notes that can be found in the Small Shack that is not in ruins. That building contains 5 letters in total:

-  Ludin's Reports (3 total notes)
-  Priest Varloo's Note
-  Priest Tyluig's Note

The notes reveal that the Hub was once part of the Holy Nation but the Shek Kingdom's forces takenover this region, disposed the corpses of previous inhabitants, and occupied the area for sometime before eventually sacking the city and set it ablaze before retreating.

After that, a Holy Nation squad was sent to the ruins to investigate. They initially found no enemy presence as the Shek Kingdom has deserted the region, remarking that "Retaking the Hub would be effortless but resettling it will prove most difficult.", presumably as a result of the level of devastation and death of this city.

In the 2nd report sometime after the 1st one, they spotted "shadowy figures" in the city, a bonedog escaped the leash of their owner and went chasing after the shadowy figures but went missing during the chase.

In the 3rd report, they went to explore The Hub during the cover of night, hearing the voice of both a man and a woman both of which vanished once they entered the gates. After losing one of the Sentinel to some unseen force picking them off, the Holy Nation soldiers retreated, claiming the ruins are haunted by "wraiths". ## Major Towns
Major Towns are locations where many different types of Vendors can be found. Often, Major Towns will contain more than one Bar. Major Towns tend to be large settlements with many buildings and residents, although not all of them provide the same services, and their size and population can vary wildly.

They often have a large selection of stores and multiple buildings for sale. They are also the only items on the map that can be seen at any zoom level, besides your own settlements.

    1.  The Holy Nation
The zealous fanatics of the Holy Nation inhabit these towns. They are not safe for non-human Races without Human escort. Even then you may encounter trouble with the residents and guards. Skeletons should stay away at all times, as they are killed on sight regardless of any escort.

    1.  Bad Teeth
-  A fortress city guarding a mountain pass between the fertile valley of Okran's Pride and hostile wilderness of the Skinner's Roam. Dust storms and hungry bandits laid waste to the land east of Bad Teeth, but the city remains a place for respite and safety.

    1.  Blister Hill
-  The glorious capital of the Holy Nation and the seat of Holy Lord Phoenix. The areas around here are patrolled heavily by The Holy Nation, and as such are relatively free of any bandit presence. If you are a full Human squad, the very fertile areas around here are great for setting up a base.

    1.  Stack
-  A large town just southeast of the center of the map, on the edge of The Holy Nation's territory. It has a decent variety of shops, and its surrounding areas often have Starving Bandits, Wild Dogs, and Dust Bandits. With all of the relatively weak enemies it makes for a decent training area for newer characters. Its proximity to the Western Hive and The Hub makes it a good location for aspiring traders and thieves alike.

    1.  Shek Kingdom

    1.  Admag
-  The Shek capital and home of the Stone Golem. It is located on top a hill in the northern part of the Stenn Desert. It features almost all types of shops and bars. There also is a Shinobi Thieves tower. Many 10 percent quality Copper mines dotted outside town.

    1.  Squin
-  A town nestled in a narrow canyon. It is located in the western part of the Border Zone. There are two  bars alongside most basic shop types. The town has relatively safe access to high quality Iron and Copper resource nodes.

    1.  United Cities

## World's End
World's End is a settlement resembling a very large Waystation found on a mountain top in the Arm of Okran.

It is currently controlled by the Tech Hunters, while also serving as the home of the Machinists faction. The latter have their University based in the town, which happens to contract most of the local Tech Hunters. The Shek Kingdom pacifier can also be found here.

Two statues resembling Okran or Phoenix is found at the southern gates, the official reason given by Finch is that they are used to appease and convince The Holy Nation that they are faithful despite the large technological cache inside the towns. It is unclear if The Holy Nation actually believes this pretense of faith given their zeal in inquisition, or deliberately turns a blind eye to this location for unknown reasons.

Holy Construction Traders in particular are aware of World's End presence, as well as their technological cache.

Hired ninjas act as the local guards. Although there is no prison in town, some form of Empire law loosely applies, as UC bounties are issued to troublemakers. These bounties are not actually enforced in town; many of the Tech Hunters themselves also have UC bounties.

A Map to World's End can help find this location.

    1.  Lore
World's End, as the home base of the Tech Hunters, offers one of the largest stores of machine and science items within Holy Nation territory.

Finch, the head of the University of Machinist, details that despite being within Holy Nation territory, and the prevalence of Okran statues at the town, the town is totally independent from the Holy Nation. He argues that the statues were sufficient enough to trick the gullible Holy Nation authorities into leaving the town alone. Because of this, one can find a large number of Skeletons within its walls, whereas in any part of the Holy Nation, just about all Skeletons are killed on-sight.
