## Abandoned Stave
' is an Artifact in Monster Train that can be found in the Concealed Caverns.

    1.  Lore
After Seraph invaded Hell, Fel left me to support his cause much more eagerly than I'd expected. Before she left, Fel gave me this stave to assist in the fights ahead. I never saw her again.
    1.  Strategy
Despite the apparent risk in taking Abandoned Stave, this Artifact can provide a massive output boost to a surprising number of strategies, which are able to take it without the Vengeful Shards slowing down them too much. Due to the opportunity cost of taking  Fel's Remorse compared to  Herzal's Compound after a boss, consistent strategies in Monster Train usually sculpt decks assuming that the run will be played on Ember unless there is something else in the run powerful enough to justify taking Fel's Remorse.

Abandoned Stave incurs much less opportunity cost as it is a standalone event, competing only against the other event option of taking Calcified Embers that eventually become converted into Excavated Embers. Compared to that option, whose power manifests much later, Abandoned Stave provides immediate value at the cost of some consistency, allowing you to play the cards that you draw much more easily. It is much stronger at high Covenant Ranks due to the presence of two Deadweight cards in the starting deck, as it will provide an immense Ember boost with the option to remove the Vengeful Shards later if the 5th Ember is deemed unnecessary.

However, Abandoned Stave is not always an auto-take, as the consistency hit can negatively affect some strategies to the point of losing the run in the short-term. If your run does not need extra Ember and cannot afford the possibility of bad turns, it may be appropriate to skip. If your run is powerful enough to accept an even greater consistency hit and is simply looking for faster acceleration or Incant triggers in the late-game, it may make sense to take the Calcified Embers instead.

Abandoned Stave also increases further in value if you have taken the Mysterious Figure event, providing another Ember increase due to the Dante's Candles, though beware that the Blight density will be very high in your deck. It will not, however, provide extra Ember for Blights shuffled into the deck during combat, such as with Dante the Deceptives Essence or the Seraph the Diligent fight.

## Ashes of the Fallen
' is an Artifact of the Hellhorned  in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
According to the Hellhorned, the dead are never really gone. Their remains are cremated and used as warpaint to imbue warriors with great strength and to remind them of their eventual return to the ground.
    1.  Strategy
' makes Summon abilities trigger an additional time, which on a standard run means that such effects are doubled in potency.

s strength obviously depends on the amount of Summon abilities your deck has, but it doesn't take very many of them for this to be a good Artifact for your deck, and if you lean into such effects, ' can easily win the entire run for you. If you see this and use Summon abilities in any significant way, you will almost always want this Artifact. It is worth speculatively taking at the beginning of a run even if your deck lacks Summon abilities unless your deck has more pressing needs.

' mostly affects Imps, so Imp-cicle, Queen's Tail, and other things that benefit Imps will naturally synergize with ' as you will want Imps to play a prominent role in your run with this.

It is worth noting that there are a few other Summon abilities and essences aside from imps in other clans (The Last Divinity (DLC) only) - list here - that are worth keeping an eye out for with this Artifact, as they have ways to become quite strong with this Artifact.

## Capricious Reflection
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
Waters touched by fragments of the lost Divinities show us reflections of another world. A world where the Divine Mother governed over the creation of life and the responsibility of death. Given the purview of her powers, it follows that her destruction created the many immortal beings of Heaven and Hell and led to our current predicament.
    1.  Strategy

' gives all drafts an upgrade, saving you lots of Gold over the course of a run, allowing you to visit fewer Merchants, and letting you draft more cards, as doing so does not dilute your deck's upgrade density.

You will need to be flexible as to what strategy you use to use this Artifact to its full potential, as it can effectively reduce your card choices by offering cards with upgrades that don't make sense, such as as Bounty Stalker with a Runestone.

If obtained early in a run, ' allows you to have a bigger deck than other runs, since it gives you one upgrade per card you add, and you will be offered several spells with a Keepstone upgrade, letting your deck have a focus despite its larger size. This also means you will likely want to plan on taking Herzal's Compound after Ring 3 and 6, since Holdover spells can otherwise limit your ability to cycle through the deck.

Capricious Reflection is occasionally taken if no substantially superior alternative is offered. Its upside in offering powerful upgrades for free (Multistrike on core units, Doublestack on utility spells) may outweigh its downside of non-optimal upgrades. Beyond Ring 4, however, the artifact is typically skipped, as one typically devises a mainline strategy by that point in the game. The upgrades that Reflection may offer from then on are either inconsequential or negative. Generally, Capricious Reflection, if taken early, sculpts a run through its upgrade distribution, causing players to lean towards units and spells with favorable upgrades. Beyond a certain point, however, the artifact provides negligible value or can complicate card drafts negatively. ￼

    1.  Additional Information

' can only apply legal, meaningful upgrades to cards. This means that some cards that have few such upgrades, like Preserve (which can only be given a Keepstone) will tend to have the same upgrades.

' can apply clan-specific upgrades to units even if you are not playing that clan - e.g. you may see a Horned Warrior with a Spikestone even if you are playing Hellhorned/Stygian.

## Channelheart
' is an Artifact of the Awoken  in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
Before the Edge Priory became the reclusive group they are now, they would channel their energy into steel lockets that could be worn by their Awoken Hollow protectors, allowing the Hollows to gain more strength at the cost of their longevity.
    1.  Strategy
' gives Sting spells +20 Magic Power, which under normal circumstances increases their damage from 10 to 30.

s use clearly depends on how many Sting spells you have over the course of your run (see this for a list of ways to have more Stings), giving you +20 damage for each. Since Sting spells tend to hit the front enemy unit, which has enough Health to tank even boosted Sting spells except perhaps in Rings 1-3, this Artifact does not often change the complexion of a run that much, though its damage adds up provided you have enough Stings.

Be careful not to over-flood your deck with Sting spells even with this Artifact, as while Sting is "free" up to a point thanks to its +1 card draw next turn effect and Ember cost, if your deck has too many of them, you may not be able to take advantage of all the draw thanks to the 10 card hand limit, and even with the +20 Magic Power boost, you do not want Sting to replace other cards lategame.

## Cheater's Hand
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
Rumblings of Seraph’s moral absolutism had become frequent in the time before Heaven’s invasion. Transgressions were rarely tolerated and often met with swift physical punishment. I can see now that his invasion of Hell was largely driven by some personal vendetta against me and my failings.
    1.  Strategy
Using Cheater's Hand effectively is not just about the extra draw- putting a card on the top of your deck is a powerful ability. It is often tempting to put Deadweights or other useless cards back but this only leads to them being drawn next turn. Instead, place a card that will be useful next turn.

 can serve as Permafrost on a card by continuously putting it on top of the draw pile. Consumer of Crowns and Furnace Tap are examples of cards that benefit a lot from this.

 becomes a lot less useful if you have cards/artifacts that cause you to draw mid-turn such as Winged Steel, Mask of Penumbra, Excavated Ember, or Offering Token, as you will often end up drawing the thing you put on top of the deck anyway (though in the case of the latter two, you can put those cards on top of your deck to line them up with turns where you need them). It is still moderately useful with these things, as you know what you will draw with them.

Channelsong or Prism Retrieval are extremely effective as you can place a unit from your hand on your deck, ensuring it receives the buffs.

## Collection of Tails
' is an Artifact of the Hellhorned  in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
An imp officially matures into a demon when their tail falls off and they learn to stand on their own feet, but this evolution only seems to enrage the demon even further. I don't like the sight of the disembodied tails, but keeping them is necessary.
    1.  Strategy
' prevents Rage from decaying after each round of combat on your units.

' obviously depends on you using Rage, but even if you do it tends to be a low-impact Artifact overall. It saves one Rage per unit that has Rage per turn, meaning that it is at its best when you are able to apply Rage to a lot of units, but even if you are able to apply Rage to all units, it cannot overtake Worn Grindstone until turn 4 unless you have Resonant Shard or Deranged Brute.

In theory it could be useful in a long fight against a Relentless boss since otherwise Rage decays by 1 per round of combat, but in practice this won't be too useful past the first few Rings, as a run that wants to apply Rage wants to apply a lot of it, decreasing both the number of times that this Artifact gets to save a point of Rage (by shortening combat) and the relative usefulness of that (saving Attack isn't nearly as useful on a unit with Attack as one with Attack).

As such, it's a fairly low-impact Artifact overall, and should only be taken with Rage synergies and a lack of a better option.

## Consumed Crown
' is an Artifact of the Hellhorned  in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
When a Hellhorned defeats another in battle, they will break off the loser's horns and add it to their collection, forming them into a crown. I've learned the hard way that asking to wear another's crown is considered incredibly rude.
    1.  Strategy
' provides Attack on Slay for friendly units, providing a little additional scaling of the course of a battle.

s effect helps you scale over the course of a battle, but with the catch that your units need to be already capable of killing enemies to do so. The fact that taking advantage of it well usually requires a high Attack to begin with and its relatively small bonus of Attack means that it tends to not be that impressive in practice, being more of a "win more" Artifact than one that can help you win a run. It is at its best with units with Multistrike, as they are both more able to take advantage of the bonuses and are often able to kill multiple units per turn, letting them scale better with this Artifact.

If you are paired with a clan that has units with Sweep or other ways to hit Support-class enemies in the back of waves, they appreciate this Artifact thanks to their ability to kill the more frail units in the back. No Hellhorned units except for Molting Imp's Summon ability are able to do this - Molting Imp's ability can trigger Slay triggers, including that of '. You can infuse Molting Imp into another unit on The Last Divinity (DLC) and have ' trigger off of the Support-class enemies killed on Summon for a decent initial power boost to that unit in some fights, though you'd probably need other synergies with this to make it worthwhile.

Overall, ' is a relatively low-impact Artifact, but it usually contributes something to most runs.

## Cursed Vines
' is an Artifact of the Awoken  in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
Not all the Edge Priory endeavors have been successes. Their attempts to return to the physical form of an Animus have been met with countless failures. The vines they've used in these experiments wither with the unstable energy that's cursed them.
    1.  Strategy

' is quite useful early in the game, allowing you to consistently clear out back line enemies every turn.

' is also helpful for Floor Overstacking strategies with several bulky units, as you can play the Vine Grasp spells it produces on your own units to pull them to the front, allowing you to spread out damage across the whole floor. It can also allow you to play big units on a different floor, use a spell to ascend/descend that unit, then use Vine Grasp to move it to the front of the floor.     1.  Strategy

Due to the effect of this Artifact, each will deal 120 damage when played and then disappear from the deck. This provides some immediate value at the cost of slowing down your core strategy going into the next fight. However, because the Calcified Embers all cost Ember, it can be difficult to play all of them out in a single combat.

Generally, you should exercise caution when deciding whether or not to take Ember Stasis unless you have Sinner's Salve to guarantee that you play out the Embers, or unless you have such a lack of frontloaded damage that being able to deal 120 damage for Ember will actually help you into the next combat. Thus, Ember Stasis is generally a below-average pickup for most runs.

The value of Ember Stasis increases, however, if you have Seraph the Diligent as your Ring 8 boss, as he shuffles Vengeful Shards into the deck at the start of combat. Being able to weaponize Vengeful Shards against Diligent's tricky waves can be a significant advantage in that fight, especially given that the Blights and Scourges generated can slow down your strategy significantly and make it difficult to break frontline tanks. Additionally, some enemies in The Last Divinity combat in the DLC also spawn Vengeful Shards when killed, further making it a better consideration in that environment.

    1.  Strategy

' generates a random Imp card every turn as long as your hand is not full, ensuring that you have a unit to block every turn and a variety of other benefits.

Imp Pool

' is usually a solid early-game pickup, as the random Imp you get each turn blocks an attack from an enemy if nothing else, and will provide you with some Rage from Fledgling Imp, some situationally useful damage from Queen's Impling or Molting Imp, extra durability from Welder Helper, Ember from Pyre Chomper, or occasionally something crazy from Transcendimp. Ashes of the Fallen powers up every Imp you get from this, and Queen's Tail effectively makes the imps free Ember-wise and give you an extra card draw each turn to boot.

' is a strong Artifact if picked up early, and there are ways to keep it relevant lategame too. It should usually be skipped if found later and your run isn't able to keep the imps cycling, either because of the Imps' Ember cost or because it cannot kill off the Imps.

## Iron Dropcage
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
As invasions of the Boneshaker have become more frequent, I’ve devised a number of traps and devices to subdue any unwanted guests. These cages should hold them back temporarily when they board the train.
    1.  Strategy
Iron Dropcage is a situational Artifact, since its value is tied directly to whether or not you have access to cards that Ascend or Descend enemies. If it's shown late in the run and you don't have movement cards yet, it should usually be skipped. However, it is extremely powerful if you can enable it, as it weaponizes your movement cards into stronger backline reach, and even sometimes provides an entire defense solution against Major Bosses for your run.

Dazed is an incredibly powerful debuff, completely disabling enemy actions and allowing your units or Pyre free rein to swing into them without retaliation. You can completely neuter dangerous backline units such as Absolvers and Purifiers while making them easier pickings for your units, or Ascend enemies you would not otherwise be able to kill on your main floors into the Pyre room to kill them off. With a high enough density of movement cards in your deck, or size=20px on a card with such an effect, you can even juggle around Major Bosses, stacking enough Dazed to disable them in Relentless long enough for otherwise defensively vulnerable floors to crush them. Be careful with your movement cards once Relentless starts, as you will now be able to Ascend the boss into the Pyre, almost guaranteeing a loss.

It is not always correct to prospectively take Iron Dropcage early (in Rings 1-2) due to its situational nature, with said decision depending on the clans in your runs:
- If you are playing as Hellhorned or Melting Remnant, which have movement cards in their Common card pool (with the former also having Tiresome Climb available later), Iron Dropcage can be strong even if you haven't seen those cards yet. Note that Common cards stop appearing after the Ring 2 card draft.
- Although they only have access to movement through one Rare card in Siren's Song, the Stygian Guard can obtain some value out of Iron Dropcage. Disabling enemies for two extra turns in the Pyre can make a huge difference against tanks, and it can also potentially stack 5 Dazed in a single go against flying bosses.
- Awoken and Umbra do not make as good uses of it as their movement cards are weaker, although Restoring Retreat can be a decent emergency solution against Seraph the Patient.
- Wurmkin has no in-clan access to movement cards, and thus Iron Dropcage should often be skipped if your other clan does not get consistent usage out of it.

    1.  Additional Information
' works even if the Ascend/Descend effect doesn't actually work on its target, such as when they are Rooted, on the bottom floor when Descended, on a pre-Relentless flying boss on the 3rd floor, or The Last Divinity.

## Light of Seraph
' is an Artifact and Major Enhancement  gained after beating Ring 3 and Ring 6 in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
The coveted Light of Seraph. Those from above worship it as much as we value the Pyre. From what I’ve seen it’s powerful, sure, but it’s not as special as they say. That doesn’t mean I won’t take some if given the chance.
    1.  Strategy
Light of Seraph is a situational boss gem. Extra Capacity on every floor is a strong boon, allowing you to assemble stronger floors and replay units with Summon or Extinguish triggers more comfortably. However, you should only take it if you know with certainty that your strategy will use that extra Capacity to the fullest extent, as the gem does not provide any immediate boost to your deck performance by itself compared to the overall excellent Herzal's Compound (which should be your default choice) or even the similarly situational Fel's Remorse. View Light of Seraph as a "tax" needed to allow these powerful strategies to function, rather than something that will always add value to your run when taken.

Some examples of situations where you would take Light of Seraph include:
- Having floor-wide scaling in your strategy, where one extra Capacity allows you to crucially place one more unit down and thus exponentially increase the power of the floor. This includes Incant or Inspire strategies, lines where you want to play extra Imps, floors involving Paraffin Enforcer or its infusion, etc.
- In the DLC, wanting an infusion that increases your unit's Capacity in exchange for a very powerful effect. Such examples can include a self-infused Awoken Hollow or Shardsoul Carver, or Shadowsiege into a 2-space carry unit, which would both produce Capacity units. In the absence of other solutions, you must take Light of Seraph to place most other Champions or Banner Units on that floor.
- In the DLC, you are playing an Umbra strategy that requires Morsels to function but have no solution for The Last Divinity's Sweep on top floor. At higher Covenant levels, the middle floor has only Capacity, forcing you to take this gem to be able to feed Morsels to your carry unit on the middle floor.

It may be tempting to take Light of Seraph even if you do not experience a massive surge in output from doing so, to play units out more easily across all three floors. However, this can prove to be a costly mistake at high Covenant ranks or against The Last Divinity in the DLC as your units must scale effectively to be able to meet the demands of the harsher difficulty. Extra Capacity does nothing on its own to help in that regard, as enemies will simply overrun weak floors regardless of how many units you have in play on them. In runs or clan combos where you do not have floor-wide scaling available, it is often correct to instead limit your play to a few units and build your line around having the starting Capacity.

## Rationing Scales
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
By the time you read this, I hope that Hell, or what remains of it, remains a united front against their invaders. But this was not always the case. The destruction of Divinities had left souls to wander endlessly and the absence of Judgement meant that all realms felt the effects of overcrowding and resource shortages. In some ways, this time before the war was even more trying than the ensuing violence.
    1.  Strategy
' sets your Pyre Health to Pyre Health as you start a Battle, giving you Gold for each Pyre Health lost if your Pyre health is above 50.

' can serve several functions:
- 1) to heal your Pyre after it takes damage in battles
- 2) to replace your Pyre heals with Gold gains.
- 3) to help increasing your score.

Function 1) is useful at the beginning of a run if your starting deck is weak, allowing you to focus on the Boss and not have to worry about steady health loss from leaked enemies. If offered ' early in a run, take it if you need it to survive the first few Rings. Do be mindful that ' should become obsolete by the end of the run if you use it for this, as otherwise you will find that later Rings will have no problem dealing 50 damage to your Pyre, especially in The Last Divinity (DLC) with Pact Shards.

Function 2) is usually fairly low-impact (and whether it helps or hurts is debatable) - it means that taking Pyre Remains gives you Gold instead of gaining Pyre Health, and Gold after Major bosses instead of gaining Pyre Health.

However, with a few specific supports, it is possible for ' to amass significant amounts of Gold. If you have Refracting Lenses and lots of Consume cards, or a Bone Dog's Favor with Permafrost, or a spell upgraded with one variant of the Heaven's Finest upgrade with Permafrost, you can potentially heal your Pyre up to Pyre Health every battle and gain a significant amount of Gold.

Function 3) is to use the artifact to gain money that helps a player in ultimately raising their score. As pyre damage only reduced score within a fight, the lost pyre health does not reduce score, however the increased money gained can help collecting score, either by buying upgrades that help to kill bosses faster, such as in a flying phase or by being available until the end adding score.

This is fairly rare, however, so overall this Artifact is most likely to be useful for early-game support when needed, and should usually be skipped in other situations.

    1.  Strategy
' restores Pyre Health whenever a card with Consume is played.

How valuable ' is depends on how many Consume cards you have - in most cases, this is worth Pyre Health per Battle, per card. If you're struggling early but have even 2 or 3 cards with Consume, this can help you survive the early game.

As a run goes on, you often get more Consume cards, increasing the amount of healing that ' provides. If you have an abundance of Consume cards (as the Wurmkin clan in particular does well), it's possible to depend on ' as your solution to Heavy-class enemies, simply letting them reach the Pyre and then using this to heal off the damage, provided you can take care of the Boss with strategies that work well against Relentless. If you have Rationing Scales (DLC only), you can turn the Pyre Health this gives you into Gold in each combat, which in Consume-heavy decks can allow it to produce a lot more value.

Overall, ' is at its best if you need health early and have Consume cards, and tends to be a low-to-medium impact Artifact otherwise, though it can sometimes be part of your strategy if Pact Shards aren't part of the picture.

## Sketches of Salvation
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
There was little time to act after Seraph’s invasion, but there was just enough for me to design a way to protect what would remain of the Pyre after its inevitable destruction.
    1.  Strategy
' very much alters the course of a run when obtained, for better or for worse. It gets you off to a fast start in a fight, playing units without spending Ember or drawing cards to play them, and gives you an easy way to Overstack your second floor, but also gives you no control over the arrangement of those units, or even what units are played on the 2nd floor.

It is often worth skipping if offered, even for 25 Gold if in a Trial, as its effect can be very detrimental for certain decks.

If you take ', your deck should consider how to handle these problems:
-  The random order means that frail units like Lodestone Totem may be put in front, jeopardizing their safety.
-  You may not have enough units for the 1st or 3rd floor when the situation calls for it. (Beware Haste in particular)
-  You have no control over which units are played; it may play Train Steward, Imps, or a key support unit. (One way to address this is to have exactly 4 non-Champion units.)
-  Your Champion will never be played by this, so they may be separated from your other units - often you will be unable to reliably pair them with a particular other unit.

Effects that change unit positioning like Hidden Passage, March of Shields, Vine Grasp, or Dripfall help address many of these problems.

Decks that build around ' can be powerful if they address these issues because of the sheer card and Ember advantage it provides (especially if units with difficult play requirements like Shadowsiege are played using it), so consider carefully if the benefits are worth the risks.

## Split Anvil
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
After completing the Rail, I destroyed the anvil I used to create all the Railspikes as a symbolic ending to this endeavor.
    1.  Strategy

', after playing a spell, reduces the cost of all spells in hand that cost less than the Ember spent on that spell to Ember for the turn.

s effect sounds strong, and it can be, but first consider its limitations:
- You needs a spell that costs Ember or more for it to do anything
- It does not apply to Unit cards, which are often what you are playing on early turns. If you spend all your Ember on a spell, you will not be able to play units in hand (unless they cost Ember)

Note that X Cost-Cost spells cannot benefit from other spells cast before it, but other spells can benefit from them, as they are considered to cost Ember equal to the Ember spent.

To make the most out of ', you will want
- lots of card draw to apply Ember to as many spells as possible (so you will generally want Herzal's Compound after Major bosses)
- a way to consistently have access to an expensive or X Cost-Cost spell (but having multiple expensive spells at the same cost does not help)
- a way to play units as well on early turns, or the ability to tolerate a slow start because you're playing units on the first few turns

Volatile Gauge pairs insanely well with this Artifact, as it checks the first two points very reliably. Forever Flame is also an excellent partner, making most units cost Ember and mostly eliminating the tension between playing units and playing expensive spells. Hell's Banners is a solid partner for similar reasons, provided you can play 2 units on early turns. Applying Holdover on an expensive spell works too, though be mindful that if you draw it and units in the same hand, you may be forced to choose between deploying the unit and playing the expensive spell with Holdover. Having Permafrost on that expensive spell as well may be a good idea. You may consider looking for upgrades that increase the cost of spells, like Doublestack, because the cost increase allows it to make other spells cost Ember.

The interaction with Tethys Titansbane's Conduit upgrade path deserves a special mention. For the purpose of comparing spell costs, the cost reduction effect of Conduit is not taken into account. This means that, for example, Conduit can reduce the cost of a Ember damage spell to Ember, and playing it will reduce the costs of all Emberand Ember spells in hand (including non-damage spells) to Ember, to be played on any floor. This can be very powerful.

' is a solid early-game pickup and can be built around to great effect. If offered later in the run, its value depends on how well your run is built to take advantage of it.

## Volatile Gauge
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
One of the first instruments I developed for the Boneshaker. Regrettably, it’s not very accurate and delivers entirely random heat output from the furnace.

    1.  Mechanics

' randomizes all non-Champion cards as they are drawn with a random cost between Ember and lets you draw +3 per turn. Costs are only randomized when a card is drawn from the deck, with created cards such as Morsels retaining their normal cost (although they will have their cost randomized if not played immediately and drawn on a later turn).

The interaction between Volatile Gauge and cost modifiers can be a bit unintuitive. Cost increases from upgrades like Stackstone or Eternalstone are ignored, but cost decreases will apply after the Gauge roll.

For example if you use Stackstone to add Doublestack and give cost +1 Ember to a Gem Trove, it will be drawn with a cost of 1-3, but if you add an Emberstone instead to give it cost -1 Ember it will be drawn with a cost of 0-2.

Although cost increases are normally ignored, if you add a cost decrease to a card that has its cost increased (for example adding cost -1 Ember to a Gem Trove with a Stackstone) the cost will still remain at 1-3 on draw.

Cost increases and decreases during battle from things like Spellchain or Archus's Blinding Darkshard will also still apply even when Volatile Gauge is active.

Another unusual interaction is with Sinner's Salve. If the player has both Sinner's Salve and Volatile Gauge, Salve will only apply cost reduction equal to the original cost of the Blight or Scourge. This means that a Weight of Contrition will be drawn with a cost from 0-2 and The Ultimate Penance will be drawn with a cost from 0-1.

    1.  Strategy

' is a very difficult Artifact to evaluate, and can either make your run much stronger or much weaker; it quite possibly could have a chance of doing both. ' both increases and decreases the variance of your run:

- it increases the variance with the random cost rolls - in particular, it exposes you to the risk that you may draw multiple crucial cards in the same turn with high Ember costs, forcing you to choose which ones to play
- it decreases the variance by giving you +3 draw per turn, ensuring that you will see and have an opportunity to play just about every card in any reasonably-sized deck within the first couple of turns, and you will get several opportunities to play any spell, which may otherwise e.g. miss the shuffle and only be seen once

In contrast to what inspired it, in Monster train, the mechanics and importance of Banner Units often mean that s risks outweigh the benefits - you will draw Banner Units early anyway, yet it's crucial to be able to play each of them no matter what they are drawn with, and there may also be important spells that you have invested upgrades into that you need to play first draw that you may draw with them too.

## Worn Grindstone
' is a Clanless  Artifact in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
As much as it pained me to see the tools of my trade used for such barbaric purposes, the war effort for Hell was in dire need of support. The forges quickly devolved into needless bickering between clans, each one claiming to be the best. Like children, I had to separate them for their own safety and my sanity.
    1.  Strategy
' gives all your units Attack, giving them a little extra punch.

' is especially helpful early in combats and early in a run, but is a generally useful Artifact that should be considered unless a more compelling option competes against it.

' does have a downside that a few runs will need to consider - it means that units that have a base value of Attack will now attack enemies, meaning they are now susceptible to Spikes on enemies when they previously were not. This can be important for some frail units like Morsel Miner, Lodestone Totem, or Wildwood Custodian, and may be a reason to skip this Artifact if those units are important to your run, as you'd need to invest in a bit of extra Health on those units with '.

## Wurmtooth
' is an Artifact of the Wurmkin  in Monster Train.

    1.  Lore
The Bogwurm is mentioned very casually among the Wurmkin. So much so that I often forgot the beast was a Titan. But it seems that the Bogwurm that gave them life and shelter is so present in their everyday routines, that their eternal devotion to study is reverence enough to the Titan. Even if they do refer to the Bogwurm as “that ghastly old worm” more than I care to acknowledge.
