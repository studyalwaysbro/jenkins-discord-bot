## Blunt Weapons
Blunt Weapons specialize in blunt damage. With the exception of the Spiked Club, blunt weapons usually cause very little bleeding damage. They require significant amounts of strength as they go up in quality (because of the increase in blunt damage).

Because stun (blunt) damage actually reduces KO time for the most part, enemies defeated with a blunt weapon will get up very quickly after getting knocked out. A character knocked out with pure stun damage will stand up seconds (if not instantly) after being knocked unconscious (unless put into a recovery coma).

    1.  Usefulness
The usefulness of blunt weapons is heavily questioned by many players. Blunt weapons offer less or the same amount of total damage as light weapons (Sabres, Polearms, Katanas) while demanding equally (if not more) strength as most Hackers, sometimes even Heavy Weapons - and not providing extra range or other benefits (the Clubs have some bonuses, but those are inferior to ones given by most hackers). However, their strength requirements combined with the popularity of Clubs can make them a potential steppingstone for early strength training. It is also worth noting that the majority of armours have a lower resistance for blunt than cut damage, sometimes quite significantly.

A commonly brought up point is that Blunt Weapons are useful for defeating bounties, because it removes the need to bandage them and reduces the chance of them dying (from bleeding and wound degeneration). However, this claim has significant flaws:

-  Killing a bounty before being able to bandage them is already very unlikely unless using an incredibly skilled characters with high damage weapons.
-  The short KO time can make it harder to actually pick up the bounty in the middle of a fight.
-  You have to either do a lot of micromanaging to only attack the bounty with a blunt-wielding character or equip an entire squad with blunt weapons. Crossbows also cannot be used.
-  You will have a harder time trying to defeat the bounty (and their crowd of supporters, which bounties often have) with blunt weapons compared to other weapon classes.

Given the downsides of blunt weapons and their prevalence among many bandit NPC factions, it can be speculated that this weapon type primarily serves as a way of balancing the game in the player's favor, similarly to how many enemies wear armor with bad coverage.

## Crossbows
Crossbows are Kenshi's only ranged weapons that can be carried. To use a crossbow, equip the crossbow, make sure that the character has the correct ammunition in their inventory, and ensure that RANGED is active under JOBS.

Crossbows can do a decent amount of damage to a single target and cause significant bleeding. It is important to note that the size of the target (and its hitbox) plays an important role in the accuracy of crossbows. As such, large animals are easier to hit than humanoids, but so also it's more difficult to hit small targets like spiders. Players should to be cautious using crossbow characters and especially positioning them with particular care during fights, it's not rare for high-powered crossbows to kill weaker enemies, and so too allies succumb to friendly fire, outright and permanently.

NOTE: There is an intentional mechanic from the developers that makes characters with the Diplomat tag "immune" to crossbows. Attempting to shoot units who have a Diplomatic status will usually result in your character aiming at someone else entirely. It is possible to have your marksmen target sometimes glitching and instead hitting an invisible target behind the Diplomat you set to attack. Despite the immunity, your marksmen can have a chance of 'accidentally' hitting the Diplomatic character in front of the unit you are aiming at. On occasion (Sometimes after stealing something from them, assassination attempt etc...) you will be able to shoot to Diplomatic targets with a Crossbow.

Crossbows can be crafted through the Crossbow Smith stat.

See Turrets for their immobile counterparts.

## Hackers
Hacker Weapon Skill description

Hackers are a class of weapons that specialize in a balance between cutting and blunt damage. These weapons all deal additional damage to Robots and have some of the highest Armour Penetration values in game, at the expense of slightly lower damage against Animals.

The Hackers skill determines damage with weapons classified as such. Only the Short-Cleaver can be equipped in the secondary weapon slot.

## Heavy Weapons
Heavy weapons require two hands to fight with.  If a character has one arm disabled he will switch to using his secondary weapon if he has one.

Heavy Weapons weigh more than most weapons and use a move set more centered around fighting large groups of units. Heavy weapons require brute force and deal heavy blunt damage. A heavy weapon will typically inflict more damage than other weapons but if you lack the strength to use them you will attack and block much slower. Due to their reach paired with their move set, when surrounded heavy weapons are very useful for hitting multiple enemies with each swing.

    1.  Weapon Skill - Heavy Weapons
Heavy Weapon Skill descriptionpos3 = Skeleton P4MkII

    1.  Speed of use
Like all weapons, attack speed, with heavy weapons is determined by a combination of having enough Strength to use it effectively, Dexterity, as well as Melee Attack. Block speed is determined by a combination of having enough Strength to use it effectively, Dexterity, as well as Melee Defense. The base attack speed is 0.765, every level in Dexterity will increase this by 0.00228, and 0.00152 for attack. Strength comes into play in the required strength level of the weapon, if below its required level your combat speed will be penalized (up to -47.5% at 20 levels or more below). Strength above the required level does not affect speed at all, beyond acting as a buffer for injuries penalizing your strength. The strength requirement to use a weapon is the blunt damage multiplied by 40.

    1.  Locations
Heavy Weapons are common among Sheks and Skeletons, but rare everywhere else. You can buy Planks and Fragment Axes in Shek Kingdom weapons shops. Low quality ones can be found in ruins in Border Zone and Shek Kingdom, as well as animal nests. Shek bandit factions Berserkers and Kral's Chosen often wield ancient quality Heavy Weapons, making them a good source of heavy weapons in the early game.

Heavy weapons are also wielded by Broken Model 30 Skeletons and Skeleton Drifters. Falling Suns are fairly uncommon, usually found as Artifacts, in select few shops (e.g. Scraphouse) and on a few NPCs.

## Katanas
Katanas are a class of weapons that specialize in cutting damage. They are a very commonly found weapon type, and their low weight makes them easy for beginners to use.

Katanas are offensive weapons, featuring attack bonuses and defense penalties, as well as a high blood loss multiplier. A major characteristic of Katanas are big penalties for armour penetration and robot damage, making them a poor choice for the latter stages of the game. However, they are quite useful against early-game enemies, who are predominantly humans in little to no armor.

They feature a two-hit combo attack (called Downward Combo) that makes them particularly useful when fighting animals or singular weak opponents but leaves their wielder vulnerable to getting hit in battles with multiple foes. The combo attack is the only weapon animation which has less than 100 power (100% of the weapon's damage). It makes up for this by attacking twice: the power of the first strike is 80 and the second one is 60. Unfortunately, though, Downward Combo is usually too slowDoes this apply to higher skill levels? to land the second hit on an opponent if they were staggered by another attack. This leads to overall less damage over time when compared to weapon types like Sabres or Polearms.

## Polearms
Polearms are a class of weapon that excels at speed and reach. Most have bonus damage against animals. Polearm and Heavy Polearm are notably the only weapons which provide an armour penetration bonus without having high strength requirements.

All Polearms have moderate attack bonuses and indoor penalties. Polearms also have the second highest blood loss multipliers out of all weapon classes (bested only by Katanas, which also have significant downsides).

They are an excellent choice for low-skilled characters, as they have a very low strength requirement and a move set similar to Katanas and Sabres while providing similar damage and much higher range. In particular, Polearm not only has the highest animal damage multiplier but also a high armour penetration. However, Heavy Polearm has a significantly lower (sic!) strength requirement and provides higher base damage, possibly making it a better option for general use.

## Sabres
Sabres are a class of weapons that specialize in heavy cutting damage with an added splash of blunt damage.

Sabres are generally defensive weapons, most featuring a defensive bonus in exchange for an attack penalty. Their attacking animations are rather fast which leaves fewer openings for others to exploit. It's not rare to see a skilled Sabre wielder stun locking their opponent if they get pushed into a corner.

## Weapons
Catun Scrapmaster
Combat is a primary part of Kenshi, and all combat except for martial arts is done with weapons. Weapons are items that are split into multiple categories, each dealing different types and amounts of damage. Armour is designed to reduce damage and can be slightly penetrated by using certain weapons. Weapons can be looted, purchased from a Weapons Trader, or crafted by the player.

    1.  Equipping Weapons
Characters in Kenshi have two weapon slots: Weapon I (primary slot) and Weapon II (secondary slot). All weapons can be equipped in the primary slot; however, weapons may only be equipped in the secondary slot if they possess no larger than an Inventory Height of 1 and an Inventory Width of 7. Characters will typically use what is equipped in Weapon I over Weapon II unless penalties are applied, such as the left arm being unusable or using certain weapons indoors. If the right arm is unusable, the character will not use equipped weapons. Multiple weapons may be equipped within Weapon I, but the character will only use the topmost weapon in that slot in combat. The bonus stats (and debuffs) will only apply for the weapon currently being used.

    1.  Melee Weapons
These are weapons which make use and train a character's Melee Attack and Melee Defense stats, as well as the specific melee weapon skill associated with the weapon type.

    1.  Ranged Weapons

## Backpacks
If a character keeps food in his backpack then it will be automatically shared with the rest of the squad.
Backpacks are carrying equipment worn on the back. When equipped, it gives the unit extra space, and depending on the bag, the ability to stack Trade Goods that normally would not stack, but at the cost of combat stats.

For Garru and Bull backpacks, the stack multiplier is applied directly. For Trader and Wooden backpacks, stack size is first set to a minimum (2 for Trader and 3 for Wooden), if its lower than minimum, and then its multiplied by stack multiplier of 3x.

Backpacks are obtained differently depending on their category. Normal Backpacks are widely for sale in Travel and Repairs Shops. Thieves Backpacks are sometimes found at normal Travel Shops but are also sold by Shinobi Traders. Animal Backpacks are sold by Travel Shops, Farm Shops and some independent animal traders. Tech Hunter Shops offer a large selection of backpacks. Traders Backpack Large and Shopkeeper Goods Bag are not for sale and can only be obtained by stealing one off of a shopkeeper's back.

Flats Lagoon, Mourn and Squin are good locations to find a large selection of backpacks in various shops.

Occasionally you can find some of the animal Backpacks, Scavenger Basket and the Wooden Backpacks inside of containers found in town ruins such as Old Village, Expedition 4, Expedition 5, Bast and Free Settlement to name a few.

## Heavy Jitte
version number = 1.0.68

Heavy Jittes, along with their smaller counterpart the Jitte, are Blunt weapons that inflict stun damage (blunt) and extremely minor cut damage/blood loss. As Dexterity experience gained is based off the cut damage of a weapon, using either of the two variants will reward no Dexterity at all. Due to their lack of any serious cut damage this makes them somewhat useful for subduing bandit leaders and other bounties without running the risk of them dying due to wound degeneration. Please note that KO time is almost entirely based on blood loss and cut damage received, stun damage only adds time when hit under 0 health in a bodypart so characters knocked out with a Heavy Jitte may get up very quickly before you can pick them up.

They are notably carried by Samurai Police.

The Cross-version of this weapon can be found on General Hat-12 in Ashland Dome IV, who used to be the Chief of the Second Empire's police force.

This weapon has inventory dimensions of 1x9, meaning it can only be equipped as a primary weapon.

## Making a Custom Melee Weapon
See also Creating Custom Weapons about putting the weapon in game.

    1.  Tips and Facts

-  Import a vanilla weapon as a base in Blender. This gives you the correct UV mapping and orientation for the weapon in 3D space.
-  Use of vanilla UV layout is (semi)mandatory. This is because the game randomizes weapon quality in vendors and when crafting, same mesh may be used with ANY vanilla texture.
-  All player-crafted weapons in game are always of Homemade manufacturer, which determines the texture used by the produced quality.
-  Catun No 1 texture is default. The game uses it if there is no corresponding texture found for the quality. So fe. your weapon's lowest quality is 2 but your crafter fails and produces a level 1 product. No lvl 1 weapon model (texture) found, Catun 1 texture is used.
-  You can force a vendor to sell only certain manufacturer and certain quality, but for crafting, a failure is always a possibility. In practice you cannot force a certain texture on player-crafted items. Not unless you replace or remove ALL VANILLA TEXTURES (from Homemade), which would make your mod not compatible with any other weapon mod.
Polycount of a weapon mesh object must be less than 8000 vertices (tricount under 4000).

A weapon object can only have one material. Even if you have separate materials set in Blender, in Kenshi they are using only the one texture.

    1.  Texture and mesh requirements
Texture for a melee weapon should follow the vanilla UV layout. Use of vanilla UV layout is (semi)mandatory. This is because the game randomizes weapon quality in vendors and when crafting, same mesh may be used with ANY vanilla texture.

Diffuse:  .dds, 90% alpha, mipmaps enabled, binary sizing (512x512, 1024x1024 etc.) and DXT5

Normal: traditional purple normal, .dds mipmaps enabled, binary sizing (512x512, 1024x1024 etc.) DXT1

Mesh: keep it relatively low poly, under 10 000 verts. Kenshi does not allow parts for melee weapons, so one mesh & one texture only.

If you absolutely want and must use custom textures, you need to make sure the weapon can only be found, given or appear in specific vendors you made yourself. A custom textured weapon can never be crafted by the player, WITHOUT breaking the vanilla crafting system. Some mods (fe. Kathun) does this, but in general it means a mod that alters the vanilla crafting system will never be possible to use with other mods. Don't do that XD

    1.  Icons
It is best to use the auto-generate option when it comes to icons. This is because it will reflect the texture (quality) that can be randomized.

Any vertex color on the weapon mesh affects the auto-generated icon only, allowing you to be sure to have fe. a darker auto-generated icon.

-If- you are making an unique weapon, with specific quality (using forced method) you can make your weapon a non-auto-generated icon.

## Staff
attack = +8
The Staff is a long, relatively fast weapon with a short reach (For a Polearm). Please note that the "fast" part has to do with the animations assigned to all Polearms. Its moderate blunt damage and low cut damage means that it is the worst weapon to train Dexterity with. (Not including Jitte/Heavy Jitte which give 0 Dex for training with) It is often found as a weapon on farmers and various civilians.

This weapon can primarily be found in shops in The Swamp.  It is often the weapon of choice of Rebel Farmers. A Meitou grade staff is in the hands of Spider Foreman in Spider Factory.

The staff is unique among the Polearms for not having a damage bonus against animals.

The ability to make this weapon can be learned by researching Basic Weapon Smithing.

This weapon has inventory dimensions of 1x10, meaning it can only be equipped as a primary weapon.

## Topper
A Topper is a Katana Class weapon that is aimed to inflict more blunt damage and sacrifice some cutting damage. While it has the same reach as the Nodachi, it does not have the penalties or bonuses it has. This makes it a better choice for those trying to avoid them. The Topper is the only Katana without a Robotic damage reduction and also happens to have the lowest Armour Penetration debuff of the weapon class.

You can find the Meitou model of this weapon on Rhinobot in Ashland Dome II.

This weapon has inventory dimensions of 1x9, meaning it can only be equipped as a primary weapon.

## Wakizashi
defence = -2
A Wakizashi is a Katana Class weapon. This is the smallest katana, giving it a major advantage indoors. Though like most katanas it has a large penalty against armour and robots. Its low damage and lack of blunt damage make it a decent option for training Dexterity.

This weapon has inventory dimensions of 1x6. It can be equipped in both the primary and secondary weapon slots.

The Cross-version of this weapon can be found on Lady Kana, Slave Mistress Ren and Slave Mistress Grace.
